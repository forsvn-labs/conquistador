# Storyboard production vocabulary

Use a shot description that tells the producer what the viewer must see. Standard film terms are shared vocabulary, not a Conquistador-owned framework. Pair any abbreviation with enough context to make the intended crop unambiguous.

## Frame the evidence

| Task in the scene | Framing decision |
| --- | --- |
| Inspect a small control, label or material detail | Use a close crop with enough context to identify the object. |
| Understand a person's action | Include the hand/body movement and the object being used. |
| Hear an explanation | Keep the speaker and any essential evidence legible together, or cut to the evidence when needed. |
| Understand a location or relationship | Include the relevant surrounding space, people or objects. |

Common shorthand includes ECU for extreme close-up, CU for close-up, MCU for medium close-up, MS for medium shot, MLS for medium long shot, LS for long shot, WS for wide shot and EWS for extreme wide shot. State the actual visible bounds; a tag alone does not settle the composition.

## Preserve continuity

Track the visible state before and after each cut. The same action must not appear to skip a required step, change hands or produce an impossible result. Keep spatial relationships understandable when switching camera positions. When a cut intentionally changes time, location or point of view, give the viewer enough information to follow it.

An audio lead or carry-over can connect scenes, but it must not make a speaker appear to say something in a different context. Record source timecodes and edits when they affect interpretation. Do not splice separate claims into a misleading continuous quotation.

## Set time and text

Give each scene a start and end, visible action, exact text, audio cue and source asset. Use a read-through or rough preview to set duration. Hold evidence long enough to inspect it. Shorten repetition before cutting a required explanation. There is no per-platform cut frequency or mandatory loop rule.

For text, specify words, placement, font treatment, contrast, appearance and removal. Preview at the intended display size with destination overlays. Preserve captions and important conditions during crops and transitions. Use the supplied brand treatment; do not invent brand colors or a borrowed creator style.

## Finish the plan

Check the timeline for gaps, overlap and unexplained jumps. Verify that the final scene resolves the opening promise and gives a usable next action. A loop is acceptable only when the answer remains complete. Return asset paths, rights, production needs, preview checks and unresolved decisions with the shot table.
