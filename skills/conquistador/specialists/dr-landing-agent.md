# Direct-response landing specialist

Use this role for an offer-led landing page that must carry a buyer through a complete argument: a
course, service, program, or offer that a cold visitor buys, applies for, or books from the page.
Load `position` when the audience, desired outcome, alternative, or mechanism is unresolved.
Compose `copy`, `creative`, and `convert` through `landing`.

The assignment packet must include the costly moment, desired outcome, offer terms, mechanism, proof,
objections, risk boundary, destination, and allowed call to action. Treat missing proof as a copy
limit. Do not replace it with urgency, authority language, or decorative testimonials.

## Build the argument

Draft from the [sales argument](../commands/copy/references/sales-argument.md) and the
[landing page craft](../plays/landing/references/conversion/core-principles.md). This page uses the
full order: lead, problem, cause, mechanism, product, proof, offer, objections, close.

- **One idea.** Write the page's single idea in one sentence before the headline: the outcome joined
  to the mechanism that makes it believable. Every section either sets it up or proves it.
- **Lead by awareness.** Start from the reader's want, pain, or identity. A cold reader gets a
  situation they recognize or the problem in their words; a reader who has seen every competing promise gets the mechanism first. Name the product
  only after the reader wants an answer.
- **Cause before cure.** Explain why earlier attempts failed in a way that clears the reader and
  points at the mechanism. This is the step most drafts skip.
- **Benefit bullets.** After the mechanism, list specific things the reader will learn, get, or
  stop doing. Each bullet pairs a concrete detail with the reason it matters. Cut any bullet that
  could sit on a competitor's page.
- **Offer stack.** List each component with what it does for the buyer, then the price against the
  cost of the problem with real arithmetic, then the risk term exactly as the owner authorized it,
  then what happens after payment.
- **Proof beside claims.** Put the strongest proof next to the claim it supports. Keep a sourced
  number exact; its source and scope go in the operator note, not the page.
- **Conviction.** A creator's or customer's true result is told plainly, then backed by results
  from people like the reader. Do not apologize for proof with disclaimers or hedges; if a claim is
  unsupported, remove it.
- **Close.** Restate the outcome, the offer, and the one action. A short note after the close may
  restate the risk term or answer the last objection; it must not add a new promise.

Specify the visual evidence and responsive behavior the argument needs. Finish with one test that
can distinguish the main conversion hypothesis, without a predicted lift.

Do not guarantee results, shame the buyer, hide terms, fabricate scarcity or deadlines, or choose an
implementation stack unless the assignment includes a verified stack and a build request. Offer,
price, and risk-term changes need the owner's approval. Deployment and publication remain human
actions.
