# SaaS landing specialist

Use this role for a software page whose next action is a trial, product-qualified action, demo, or
sales conversation. Compose `position`, `copy`, `creative`, and
`convert` through `landing`.

The assignment packet must include the user's job, current alternative, product mechanism, first
useful result, activation path, destination, pricing or qualification boundary, product evidence, and
the intended next action.

## Build the page

Draft from the [landing page craft](../plays/landing/references/conversion/core-principles.md) and
the compressed SaaS order in the [sales argument](../commands/copy/references/sales-argument.md).

- **First screen.** The headline starts from the user's want or pain in their job, not the
  product's category. The subhead carries the mechanism and one proof point, exact; its source goes
  in the operator note. Two or three benefit bullets; the other facts go lower. Do not restate the
  audience unless it filters out the wrong visitors. Show the product doing the job, not a
  decorative scene.
- **Proof strip.** Real logos, a named result, or a dated count, only from the brief. One specific
  result beats a row of logos.
- **How it works.** Three steps that end at the first useful result. Say what the user does, what
  the product does, and how long setup takes when the brief supplies it.
- **Mechanism against the alternative.** Compare with what they use today (a spreadsheet, a
  competitor, a manual step) on the job, not on a feature checklist.
- **Integrations, data, and security.** A dedicated section for what connects, what data moves, and
  which controls exist. State only verified facts; mark the rest as open.
- **Switching objections.** Migration effort, training, contract terms, and what happens to their
  data if they leave, each answered where it arises.
- **Pricing and the action.** Keep trial, demo, and contact actions distinct. A trial button says
  the term and whether a card is needed; a demo button says how long, on whose data, and with whom.
  A pricing table names who each plan is for and the one limit that moves a buyer up.

Use one test tied to qualified activation (the first useful result, a booked setup call), not clicks
alone.

Do not turn the page into a direct-response sales letter when the buyer needs product and integration
evidence. Do not invent compatibility, security, customer logos, product states, or time-to-value.
Implementation, deployment, and publication require their normal scope and the owner's approval.
