# LinkedIn

Use for posts from a founder, an employee, or a company page: news, customer results, lessons, hiring.

## Decisions

- The lines above "see more" carry the post. Put the result, the moment, or the news there.
- A founder post sells through the person: the work they did before, what they built, what changed.
  A company post leads with the customer's result in the reader's terms and lets the customer's own
  words carry the feeling.
- Hiring: name the role, the one requirement that matters most and why, and the pay. Ask people who
  are not a fit to pass it on.
- Paragraphs of one to three sentences. Keep lists for details the reader scans, such as pay and
  location.

## Platform facts (checked Oct 2026; confirm in the composer)

- A post holds 3,000 characters. A comment holds 1,250, but a comment in a group conversation holds 1,000.
- The feed folds a post after about two or three lines. LinkedIn does not publish the cutoff.
- A PDF posted as a document shows as a swipeable carousel.

## Annotated pairs

The people and products are synthetic. Nothing here was posted.

Founder post; the product adds an export inspectors accept; goal: builders reply.

```strong
For nine years I wrote the site diary at the end of the day, in the van, from memory.

Sitecheck writes it now. The supervisor says a few words over the photos they already take, and the diary is done before they leave site: who was on, what got finished, what held the job up.

210 small builders use it. From today it also exports the diary in the format council inspectors ask for, so nobody retypes it before an inspection.

If you run a crew, what does your site diary miss most often?
```

```weak
Paperwork is killing small builders. 🚧

I know. I lived it.

Nine years. Every night. In the van.

So I built Sitecheck. Today we're excited to announce council-ready exports! 🎉

Builders everywhere are saving hours.

Agree? 👇
```

**Why:** The strong post opens on the founder's own past and shows what the product does in one
concrete sentence. The weak one stacks fragments, invents "saving hours", and asks a question nobody
needs to answer.

CEO hiring post; goal: qualified people apply or share.

```strong
If you have dispatched gritting trucks at 4 a.m. in a snowstorm, I would like to talk to you.

Gritmap plans and tracks winter gritting routes for 60 contractors. We are 22 people, and we are hiring our first Head of Support.

Our customers call when a truck is off route and the council is on the other line. Someone who has sat in a dispatch chair knows what they need in the first ten seconds. That matters more to me than support-software experience.

- Remote, UK
- £55k–£65k plus equity
- Busiest November to March

Apply at https://gritmap.example/jobs. If you know a dispatcher who would be good at this, send it to them.
```

```weak
We're hiring! 🚀 Gritmap is looking for a passionate, customer-obsessed Head of Support to join our fast-growing team. You thrive in ambiguity and love solving problems. Experience in logistics is a plus. Competitive salary. DM me!
```

**Why:** The strong post explains why one background matters, with a scene only that reader knows,
and states the pay. The weak one lists traits anyone can claim and hides the pay.
