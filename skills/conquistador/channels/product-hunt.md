# Product Hunt

Use for the listing (name, tagline, description, gallery), the maker comment, and launch-day replies.

## Decisions

- The tagline says what someone gets, in words a visitor understands without the product name.
- The maker comment is a person talking: who you are, the problem as the user lives it, how the
  product handles it, what it costs, what it cannot do yet, and one question you want answered.
- The first gallery image shows the product doing its main job. Each later image adds one step.
- Ask for feedback, not votes. Reply to every comment on the day, including the critical ones.

## Platform facts (checked Oct 2026; confirm in the Product Hunt help center)

- Name: 40 characters. Tagline: 60 characters. Description: 260 characters.
- The launch day runs from 12:01 a.m. to 11:59 p.m. Pacific time.
- Product Hunt does not allow asking for upvotes, and makers can launch without a hunter.

Tagline and maker comment for a consumer app.

```strong
Tagline: Dinners from what's about to go off in your fridge

Still throwing out half a bag of spinach every Sunday?

I'm Jun. My partner and I built Mealcount after a month of writing down what we binned. You photograph your fridge after the weekly shop. Next time you open the app, it compares the photos and suggests three dinners that use what has been there longest.

It's free for 10 scans a month, then $4 a month. It can't see inside opaque containers yet.

What would make you open it on a weeknight instead of ordering in? I'm here all day to answer.
```

```weak
Tagline: The AI-powered smart kitchen assistant revolutionizing home cooking

Hey Hunters! 👋 We're SO excited to finally launch Mealcount! It's the all-in-one platform for modern home cooks. Please support us with an upvote 🙏
```

**Why:** The strong tagline promises a result in the user's words, and the comment explains how the
app works, what it costs, and its limit. The weak tagline is too long and abstract, and the comment
asks for votes.
