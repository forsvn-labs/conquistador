# Working hypothesis review

A hypothesis connects supplied evidence or an explicit assumption to a proposed page change and
a decision test. It does not need to be exclusive to the product, use a particular metaphor, or
promise a numerical lift. Generic usability repairs can be valuable when a concrete defect exists.

Record four things:

1. **Basis:** what was observed, by whom, where, and with what limits; or label the premise assumed.
2. **Change:** the message, information, or interaction that would change and why it addresses the basis.
3. **Test:** what observation would support or challenge the change; mark missing measurement inputs.
4. **Cost and risk:** implementation dependencies, possible misunderstanding, and what could be made worse.

Mark each complete, incomplete, or unsupported with a reason. Unsupported causal claims must be
rewritten as hypotheses. Do not invent thresholds, competitor deficiencies, or audience objections
to fill the template. Missing data can lead to a comprehension check or a request for evidence.

A bounded inline request may use one working hypothesis. File-handoff can present alternatives when
there is a real choice. Alternatives must differ in what uncertainty they address, not merely color
or wording. Recommend based on evidence and cost, with uncertainty stated; no forced candidate count.

Synthetic example: the supplied page says “Download” but opens a contact form. Hypothesis: naming
the request and explaining response timing will clarify the commitment. Check a reader's account
of what happens on submission. No observed reader response or business improvement is claimed.
