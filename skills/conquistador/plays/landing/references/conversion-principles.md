# Landing-page review method

Version: `landing-decision-review-v1`. This is an original local review procedure for evidence,
visitor decisions, messages, actions, and readability. It claims no measured conversion advantage.
The previous LP-series rubric is retired; do not reinterpret historical CP scores as these LP checks.
Old briefs need a fresh review under this version.

For each applicable check report PASS, FAIL, UNKNOWN, or N/A with a brief location and reason.
Unsupported public claims and misleading actions block readiness. Missing inputs stay UNKNOWN;
a useful bounded draft can still be returned with visible gaps. N/A needs a scope reason.

## LP-01 — Reader task

Applies to: headlines.

Headlines and section titles identify the decision or information the section serves. A headline is not required to assert uniqueness or urgency. Compare alternatives only when they resolve a real uncertainty.

## LP-02 — Product explanation

Applies to: opening.

The opening explains the actual offer, intended visitor, and relevant limitation. Do not promise instant comprehension; show the exact wording a reader would use to identify the offer.

## LP-03 — Accurate action

Applies to: actions.

The action label and nearby explanation match the actual destination and commitment. A request, purchase, download, and signup must not be confused. Pronoun choice is optional.

## LP-04 — Necessary information collection

Applies to: forms.

List each requested field, why it is needed now, whether optional, and how it is handled. Remove unjustified collection. State validation, failure, and recovery behavior. There is no universal field-count limit.

## LP-05 — Entry-promise continuity

Applies to: campaign entry.

Compare the page with the supplied entry message. Preserve material price, availability, and scope conditions. An unknown traffic source is an explicit assumption, not a reason to invent an ad.

## LP-06 — Decision-answer sequence

Applies to: page narrative.

Each section answers a relevant unresolved visitor question with evidence or an explicit limit. Order answers by dependency; do not manufacture distress or require a fixed emotional narrative.

## LP-07 — Readable meaning

Applies to: all copy.

Use words appropriate to the audience, define necessary unfamiliar terms, and separate dense instructions. Check ambiguous referents and commitments. Sentence length can guide an edit but cannot prove comprehension.

## LP-08 — Capability and consequence

Applies to: feature descriptions.

Connect a described capability to an observable use and its prerequisites. Distinguish present behavior from expected benefit. Do not invent emotional or business outcomes to complete a formula.

## LP-09 — Inspectable evidence

Applies to: proof.

Each quotation, measurement, logo, and endorsement has a source, scope, permission, and relevant date. Remove unsupported proof. A placeholder is an implementation gap and must never be presented as a real customer.

## LP-10 — Voluntary choice

Applies to: decision points.

Show material costs, limits, and alternatives without invented scarcity, coerced consent, or hidden commitments. A real deadline needs its reason and source. Refusal must remain usable.

## LP-11 — Relevant reassurance

Applies to: actions and concerns.

Answer the specific uncertainty attached to an action, such as data handling or cancellation. Use only verified assurances. A badge is optional and cannot stand in for an unsupported promise.

## LP-12 — Build acceptance

Applies to: handoff.

List behavior that must be checked in implementation: destinations, state changes, keyboard flow, forms, responsive layout, and approved data handling. Distinguish planned checks from tests actually run.

## LP-13 — Comprehension check

Applies to: opening and primary action.

Ask a reader to explain the offer and the next commitment in their own words. Record who checked what and any misunderstandings. An agent self-check is not a human study or measured conversion result.

## LP-14 — Remaining uncertainty

Applies to: whole page.

List unanswered material questions, missing evidence, owners, and the next useful test. Do not fill absent results with estimates or treat all visitors as following one universal question order.

## How to apply it

The section writer records the visitor question, answer, source or assumption, and next decision.
The critic reviews the assembled argument, not a count of techniques. Cite exact brief locations
and propose the smallest repair. Do not require every check on every section. Do not convert an
UNKNOWN into PASS because a template has been filled.
