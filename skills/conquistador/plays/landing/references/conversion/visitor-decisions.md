# Visitor decisions and control

A page can help someone make an informed choice without predicting or exploiting a psychological
response. Work from the visitor's task and the information they need, not an influence checklist.

## Identify the commitment

For every action, state the immediate result and any later obligation: account creation, payment,
contact from a seller, recurring charge, or data access. Separate optional participation from what
is necessary to provide the requested service. Do not use an unrelated default to manufacture consent.

## Inspect uncertainty

Ask what is unknown at the decision point. If the answer concerns product behavior, verify it. If
it concerns policy or commercial terms, obtain the actual terms. If it concerns a visitor's
preference, offer a clear choice or test the question. A badge cannot resolve a missing contract term.

A genuine deadline or capacity limit may be stated with its source and scope. Do not create a
countdown that resets, imply a limited allocation without evidence, or make declining sound
irresponsible. Show prices and conditions where the visitor can use them before committing.

## Preserve an exit

Describe cancellation, return, refusal, and error recovery when relevant and verified. Do not invent
a guarantee to make the action feel safer. Ensure the proposed interaction allows the visitor to
understand and undo a reversible choice. Mark any unsupported recovery behavior as a build gap.

## Review the language

Remove assumptions that the visitor is afraid, wasting money, or falling behind unless the user
has supplied appropriate evidence and the statement is necessary. Prefer a supported description
of the task and limitation. Do not assign personality traits or decision styles from job titles.

Record the decision, information needed, available evidence, missing answer, and proposed repair.
This is a design review, not proof of behavior change or an empirical theory of persuasion.
