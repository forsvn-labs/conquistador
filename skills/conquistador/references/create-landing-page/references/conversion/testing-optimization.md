# Plan a test that can change the decision

State the unresolved question, proposed change, relevant audience, and observation that would
support or challenge the change. A test plan is not a result. Do not attach a universal expected
lift, sample size, duration, or confidence label to a page change.

## Before execution

Record the current behavior, measurement definition, data availability, exposure unit, known
confounders, and operator-approved decision rule. A comprehension check can answer whether a reader
understands a commitment; it cannot establish conversion impact. A traffic experiment needs a
design suitable for its volume and assignment process. If those are unknown, leave the plan incomplete.

Choose a primary outcome relevant to the actual task and guardrails for harm, such as failed forms,
unwanted contact, or poor-fit inquiries. Do not optimize a click that misrepresents the action.
Keep data collection within approved scope and privacy requirements. No instrumentation is
installed or external traffic changed as part of drafting this plan.

## After authorized execution

Record exact variant, exposure period, observations, exclusions, missing data, and limitations.
Separate measured facts from interpretation. If data are insufficient or collection failed, report
inconclusive. Do not turn a favorable direction into a causal claim or stop opportunistically without
accounting for the decision rule. Route post-launch diagnosis to the appropriate evaluation workflow.

## Synthetic example

Assumption: readers may confuse a request form with an immediate download. Proposed repair: name
the form as a request and explain the response process. First check: ask a reader what happens on
submission and record their answer. No actual reader response or performance result is supplied here.
