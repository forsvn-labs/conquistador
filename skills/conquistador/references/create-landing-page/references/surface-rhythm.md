# Arrange the page for reading and decisions

Start with the questions and dependencies in the working hypothesis. Use the supplied design system
for visual values. Do not assign universal viewport heights or infer reading speed from section size.

Choose the opening information according to the task. A visitor comparing terms may need scope and
price early. A visitor evaluating compatibility may need prerequisites. A visitor learning a new
product may need a concrete explanation before detailed evidence. These are possibilities to check
against the supplied audience evidence, not rules based on industry stereotypes.

For each section record what it answers, what prior context it needs, and why it comes next. Mark
where the visitor can act or leave and which material conditions must be available there. Use
spacing and hierarchy to separate ideas while preserving content order on smaller screens.

A calculator needs inspected logic, assumptions, and labeled limitations; it need not have unique
data to be useful. A story needs relevance to the visitor task; its page category does not determine
whether it belongs. A proof item needs permission and scope, not an arbitrary freshness cutoff.

Return a section list and a simple order diagram if useful. Explain unresolved decisions. Do not
claim the arrangement increases conversion or that a visitor follows a fixed emotional sequence.
