# Growth doctrine

Money flows where attention goes. Every growth, marketing, sales, and distribution task moves a
customer through a funnel. Use this doctrine to decide what to work on and to tie each deliverable
to a result. It never adds deliverables the user did not request. Never invent a rate, benchmark,
or result; record a missing number as `Open`.

## 1. Funnel first

Read the `## Funnel` section of `GROWTH.md` before you plan or create. When it is missing and the
task depends on it, infer the motion from the facts, mark it `Inferred`, and ask at most three of
the questions below. Do not block a small task on it.

A funnel stacks up to four parts: attention (reach and awareness), conversion (sign-up, trial,
paywall, or purchase), engagement (activation, habit, retention, referral), and sales (hand-raise,
lead, qualified, meeting, proposal, close). Revenue follows: pay, expand, renew.

| Motion | Fits when | Stages | Usual break |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product-led | Low price, fast value, user starts alone | visit, sign-up, activate, habit, pay, expand | Activation |
| Sales-led | High contract value, several deciders | reach, hand-raise, qualify, meet, propose, close | Lead quality, meeting to opportunity |
| Hybrid | Self-serve start, sales for large accounts | sign-up, activate, product-qualified lead, sales touch, team plan | Late hand-off from usage signals |
| Transactional | Buyer decides in one visit | impression, product view, cart, checkout, repeat | Product page, first-order margin |
| Consumer subscription | Ads or content bring installs | view, install, onboarding, paywall, trial to paid, renew | Paywall, early renewal |
| Marketplace | Needs supply and demand | supply joins, supply active, demand visits, match, transaction | Searches with no match |
| Audience-led | Attention is the product | reach, follow, owned-list sign-up, engage, buy | Rented followers never reach an owned list |

Price decides the motion. As a rule of thumb, yearly value per customer under about $1,000 sells
self-serve; $1,000 to $15,000 sells hybrid; above that sells through people. Check it against how
the buyers actually buy. Conversion models: free plan or freemium (pays at a limit), free trial
(pays when time ends), hard paywall (pays before full value), one-time purchase, demo and contract.

Questions that find the funnel. Ask until each answer is a fact, a number, or a named gap:

1. Who pays, and who uses? Are they the same person?
2. What does one customer pay, how often, and how (card, invoice, contract)?
3. Where does the customer first get real value, and how long after first contact?
4. Can a buyer get that value without talking to a person?
5. Which event shows that a customer will stay?
6. How did the last ten customers find you, in their words?
7. Where do most prospects stop today, and how do you know?
8. What makes one customer bring another?
9. Which numbers are measured, and which are guesses?

## 2. Name the stage

For a plan, strategy, campaign, brief, or experiment, state the funnel stage, the awareness stage,
the metric, and the expected effect. Put it where the format allows: a header, a plan table, or a
note. Keep it out of ready-to-publish copy. Skip it for small edits.

## 3. Fix the narrowest stage

The bottleneck is the stage whose gap to target, times downstream value, is largest. Score ideas
against it. Fix retention before you scale reach: when most new users leave in the first weeks,
more reach buys more churn. A quick check is the share of active users who would be very
disappointed without the product; under 40% says work on product and activation first.

## 4. Aim the product

Name who pays (demographics or firmographics), for which problem, in which ecosystem, and the
psychological need. Solve a big problem in a small way, not a small problem in a big way. A strong
market has money and attention already flowing to the problem, search demand, a value you can show
in one sentence, people who say "did you hear about this?", buyers you can reach, and optionally
recurring revenue.

Name the core desire, one of the seven deadly motivators: pride (status), greed (gain), sloth
(ease), envy (comparison), wrath (anger at the status quo), lust (desire), gluttony (abundance).
Business buyers have the same desires at work. Fear of loss, risk, or blame often decides a
business purchase; pair it with one desire.

## 5. Win attention

Results come 40% from the audience, 40% from the offer, and 20% from the copy. Build in that order.
When a campaign fails, check the audience first, the offer second, and the copy last.

### Awareness ladder

Buyers move up this ladder, from farthest to closest to a sale:

| Stage | What the buyer knows | Message job |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Does not know they have the problem | Name the problem through a situation they recognize |
| Problem aware | Feels the pain; does not know a fix exists | Make the cost of the problem concrete |
| Solution aware | Knows solutions exist; does not know your product | Show why your approach fits them better |
| Product aware | Knows your product; is not yet convinced | Answer objections with proof |
| Most aware | Knows and wants your product | Remove friction and make the offer |

Customer research finds the core desire, the pain in the customer's words, the trigger, the current
alternative, the objections, where they gather, who they trust, and their awareness stage. Use
their words.

An integrated marketing communication (IMC) plan keeps one message spine (audience, problem,
promise, proof, offer, action) and gives each channel one job: an awareness stage, a funnel stage,
and a metric. Channel families: search and AI answers, content and earned media, communities,
social and creators, stores and listings, email and SMS, real-world events, partnerships, outbound,
and in-product loops.

For a large awareness campaign, use three acts. The Pledge shows the familiar problem. The Turn
shows something that breaks what the audience believed was possible. The Prestige reveals the
product with proof and an offer, and leaves a story people retell.

## 6. Run the loop

Solve a funnel problem in order: define it as a measurable gap; list every possible root cause by
stage, segment, channel, product, and time; write falsifiable hypotheses; name the data that
proves or disproves each; analyze; then act on the surviving cause.

Grow in a loop: generate ideas, each with its stage; score Impact, Confidence, and Ease from 1 to
10 and rank by the average; write the baseline, expected lift, sample, end date, and decision rule;
run the smallest test; keep, kill, or iterate; make winners standard work.

Attribute every asset: give it a stage and a tracking tag before it ships. Judge a channel with at
least two lenses before you cut or scale it: click tracking, a self-reported "how did you hear
about us?", an incrementality test, or retention and revenue by source.

Product-led growth sells through the engagement funnel, sales-led growth through the sales funnel,
and evergreen content and community through the attention funnel. Look for the loop that feeds
the funnel: content that brings visitors who create content, users who invite users, revenue that
buys attention.
