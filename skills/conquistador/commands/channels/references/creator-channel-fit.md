# Creator Channel Fit

**Load when:** a plan considers paying creators or influencers with an existing audience to show the
product, or compares that option with creator-made ads (UGC) or paid ads.

This file decides whether the creator channel fits the niche. It does not pick creators or set deal
terms; those belong to the [creator sponsorship mode](../../outreach/references/modes/creator-sponsorship.md)
in `outreach`. Running the program belongs to the
[creator program model](../../campaign/references/distribution-models/creator-program.md) in `campaign`.

## Name what each option buys

State which of these the plan needs before you compare them:

| Option | What you buy | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creator-made ads (UGC) | Content volume for your own or paid distribution | No existing audience; most assets go nowhere |
| Paid ads | Distribution you control and can turn on or off | No reach beyond what you pay for; platform risk |
| Sponsored creators | The trust an existing audience has in the creator | Slower to scale; harder to attribute; reputation risk |

Do not pick creators because the channel is fashionable, UGC because it is cheap, or ads because they
are predictable. Pick the option whose purchase the plan needs.

## Check the niche on four conditions

Record each condition as met, not met, or unknown, with the evidence and its date.

1. **Creator supply exceeds brand demand.** Many creators reach the buyer, and few brands compete to
   sponsor them. Check which brand categories already sponsor creators in the niche.
2. **Native integration.** The product fits into content the creators already make, with no change
   of format. A viewer understands what the product does from the clip alone. A product that needs a
   long explanation fails this condition; complex or B2B products often do.
3. **Built-in reveal format.** The niche's main formats carry their own pull, for example a
   transformation, a before and after, or a progress reveal that the product can sit inside.
4. **Audience trust.** Comment sections show trust and action, not only reactions. Read the comments
   on the recent posts of several leading creators in the niche. Questions, personal stories, and
   replies between commenters show a buying audience. Mostly emoji or one-word reactions show an
   entertainment audience.

Interpret the result:

- All or nearly all conditions met: recommend sponsored creators as a primary test.
- About half met: the channel can work but needs more spend and a longer runway. Say which condition
  is missing and what it costs.
- Few or none met: recommend creator-made ads for upside or paid ads for predictability instead.

An unknown condition is a gap to close with sampling, not a pass.

## Test the niche before you kill it

Test a niche with several creators and different angles before you call it a failure. Continue when
any test shows a conversion signal. Kill the niche only when every attempt fails on the agreed
downstream outcome. Size each test so that no single deal can break the budget.

Record the reversal evidence: which observed result would move the creator channel up or down.
