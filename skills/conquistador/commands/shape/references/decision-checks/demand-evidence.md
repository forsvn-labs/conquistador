# Test the need for the proposed change

Use when the proposed value depends on another person's behavior. Name the people affected and the specific situation in which the product would help.

## Build an evidence note

Record the most recent relevant event available to the user: what happened, the current workaround, its consequence, and the source of the account. A complaint, a repeated workaround, an accepted trial, and a purchase answer different questions. Preserve that distinction.

Check whether the person who experiences the problem also chooses or pays for the solution. Identify access, procurement, migration, or trust conditions that could prevent adoption even if the problem is real.

## Select the unresolved question

Choose the question whose answer would most change the proposed scope. Examples include whether the problem recurs, whether the proposed action fits the user's work, or whether the buyer can approve a trial. Avoid requesting a general market opinion when a concrete behavior would answer the question.

Design a bounded observation:

1. Identify eligible participants and the behavior to observe.
2. State what participants will receive and what they must do. Do not imply that an unfinished product exists.
3. Set an observation window and record nonresponse, withdrawal, and failed use as well as favorable outcomes.
4. Define in advance what would justify continuing, revising, or stopping. Use context-specific criteria agreed with the user, not an imported conversion benchmark.

## Deliver

Return an evidence table with source, observed behavior, alternative explanation, and implication for scope. Follow it with the proposed test and its authority boundary. When recruitment or outreach lacks approval, provide the draft and stop before contact.

## Example, synthetic

Users report difficulty reconciling two exports. A useful test asks them to reconcile a representative, permitted sample with a proposed comparison view. Positive interview comments alone cannot establish that the view saves time. Record accuracy and effort on the task, including cases where the current process works better.

## Limits

A small trial can reveal a workflow failure. It cannot establish market size, a reliable conversion rate, or willingness to pay outside its participants. State those limits in the recommendation.
