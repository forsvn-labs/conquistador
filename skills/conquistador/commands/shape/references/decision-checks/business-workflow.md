# Check a business workflow offer

Use when the proposed product changes work inside an organization.

## Map the transaction

Identify the daily user, budget owner, approver, and person responsible when the process fails. Record the current input, output, handoffs, and recovery process. A user preference does not establish purchase authority.

Choose one representative permitted task. Describe how the proposed service would complete it, what systems it must access, and what the user would still have to do. Identify sensitive data and authorization requirements before suggesting a trial.

## Evaluate fit

Compare the proposed result with the current process on accuracy, completion time, review effort, and operational burden. Use the measures that matter to this buyer. Include integration, training, support, and exit costs in the comparison.

Check whether value depends on several teams adopting at once. If so, a single-user demonstration cannot establish organization-wide feasibility. Choose a bounded trial with the necessary participants or narrow the claim.

## Shape a feasible offer

Specify deliverables, acceptance criteria, service limits, support, and billing conditions. Separate a proposed price from an accepted price. Do not infer revenue from interest or treat an unpaid trial as a sale.

## Output

Return the workflow map, buyer and authority map, trial scope, dependencies, and evidence needed for the next commercial decision. Keep outreach and contracts in draft until authorized.

## Example, synthetic

A report generator saves preparation time but adds a manual data-permission review. Include that review in the trial timing. If the approver cannot grant access, propose a permitted sample-data demonstration and mark production fit unresolved.

## Limit

One team's task result is evidence about that task. It does not establish repeatable sales, total market demand, or a price that other buyers will accept.
