# Check machine-readable product facts

Use when people or software need to discover and interpret public product information.

## Identify the facts that affect a decision

List product capabilities, limitations, pricing terms, prerequisites, contact or support path, and source date. Assign an owner for facts that change. Distinguish public information from private instructions, credentials, and customer data.

## Inspect the available representation

Check whether the approved public page or document exposes those facts as readable text with stable links. Compare important facts across the primary page, documentation, and structured representations. A structured file that contradicts the visible offer creates ambiguity rather than discoverability.

Check access and indexing settings through authorized local inspection or approved tools. Do not promise placement in search results or model answers. Presence of a file does not prove that an external system consumes it.

## Design verification

Create representative factual questions from the product's actual offer. Verify that an authorized reader can locate the answer and identify its source. Record missing, ambiguous, and stale answers. Test external retrieval only when the required access and authority exist; otherwise provide the test procedure.

## Output

Return a fact-to-source map, discrepancies, proposed corrections, and a maintenance owner. Keep public edits as drafts until publishing is authorized.

## Example, synthetic

The pricing page lists a monthly allowance while an older guide says unlimited use. Resolve the offer with its owner and update both approved representations. Adding more machine-readable metadata would not resolve the contradiction.

## Limit

Readable, consistent facts improve interpretability. They do not establish ranking, referral traffic, or endorsement by an external search or model provider.
