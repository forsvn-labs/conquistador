# Bound the next commitment

Use before recommending build work, a trial, hiring, or a paid channel. Separate the work needed to learn from the work needed to operate a service reliably.

## Describe the deliverable

Name what will exist at the end of the next step and who will use it. List the service obligations it creates: support, maintenance, data retention, refunds, or continuing access. A manual trial is an option only if its promised service can actually be delivered.

Estimate time, direct cost, and ongoing workload from the user's resources. Mark unknown costs explicitly. Include the cost of ending the trial or moving participants back to the current process.

## Check dependencies

For each necessary tool, person, dataset, or permission, record its availability and owner. A plan that needs unapproved access must end at an access request or a substitute test. Do not describe a prepared integration as an executed one.

Choose the next commitment based on what it can resolve. Automation is justified when its implementation and maintenance serve the agreed outcome. Manual work is justified when it produces useful evidence within the available capacity. Neither is the default for every business.

## Stop and review conditions

Define the decision date, evidence to collect, maximum commitment, and stop condition. Make the stop condition actionable: name who stops the work and what happens to users, records, and pending obligations. If continuing would require a new approval, state that boundary in the plan.

## Output

Provide a compact schedule of deliverable, dependency, owner, limit, verification, and review decision. List missing authority separately from technical uncertainty.

## Example, synthetic

A proposed assisted onboarding trial can accept only the number of participants the team can support during the agreed window. Draft the enrollment terms and support schedule first. Success would justify a separate decision about automation; it would not authorize unlimited enrollment.

## Limit

A scoped plan is an estimate until the dependencies and capacity are confirmed. Do not infer profitability or readiness to scale from completion of the first trial.
