# Ad craft

Write the ad set a strong performance creative strategist would launch. Draft from this file, then
run [the check](anti-patterns.md) on the finished set. Compare drafts with the annotated
[Meta and TikTok](examples/ad-sets-social.md) and [search and LinkedIn](examples/ad-sets-search-b2b.md)
pairs. For account structure, testing, and kill rules, see [creative cadence](ad-intelligence/creative-cadence.md).

## Decide three things before you write

1. **The moment.** Where is the reader when the ad appears, and what are they doing or putting off?
   Name that moment in their words. A segment ("clinic owners") is not a moment.
2. **One angle per variation.** Each variation is a separate bet on why this reader buys. Two
   variations on one angle are one test written twice, and ad systems group near-identical ads.
3. **What each variation carries.** A person sees one variation, so each stands alone. The angle
   sets the first line and the order; under it, every variation says what the product does, gives
   one reason to believe, and states the offer terms needed to act. Other facts go in only when the
   angle needs them.

| Angle | The variation opens on | Choose it when |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | The costly moment the reader lives through | The problem recurs and the reader can picture it |
| Outcome | The day after the problem is gone | The result is easy to see or feel |
| Proof | The most surprising fact, or a customer's own words | The fact or quote is strong enough to stop a scroll |
| Objection | The doubt that stops most buyers | One doubt (price, effort, "tried that before") blocks the sale |
| Identity | Who the reader is or wants to be | The product fits a self-image the reader holds |
| Comparison | What the reader uses now | There is a clear incumbent: a spreadsheet, the phone, a rival habit |
| Offer | The terms | The price, trial, or free step is itself the news |

Pick the three to five angles most likely to win for this product. Do not fill the table.

## Open in the reader's world

- The first line must earn the rest. Name the reader's situation, a result they want, or a fact
  they did not expect. The product can arrive in line two.
- Write as the reader would think it, not as the brand would describe itself. Open with the product
  name in one variation at most.
- Describe the situation; never assert a personal trait. "Still awake after midnight?" describes a moment.
  Lines that assert the reader's health, finances, age, or similar traits break ad policy on Meta and
  elsewhere.
- A question works only when the target reader's honest answer is yes.
- Vary the shape across the set: a question, a statement, a quote, or a number, not one shape
  in every opening.

## Proof leads once, then supports

- Let proof lead in one variation at most: when the number surprises, or when a customer said it
  better than you can. In the other variations it supports the angle in a later line.
- Put a number in the reader's terms: what they gain, save, or stop doing.
- Keep a strong customer quote word for word. It often beats your own line.
- The source, sample, period, and method of a number belong in the operator note, not on the ad.
  Keep only the qualifier that truth needs, such as "the median clinic".

## The headline and the offer

- The headline gives a reason to click: the outcome, the offer, or the next step. It is not the
  product category or a description of features.
- State the terms a buyer needs to act (price, free trial, free scan, returns) in every variation:
  as the news in an offer variation, as support in the others. The action matches the landing page.

## The visual carries the angle

Give each variation its own visual. A pain variation shows the moment; a proof variation shows the
quote or number; a demonstration shows the product doing the job in the first seconds. Write the
visual direction as one shot a designer or creator can make, with captions for sound-off viewing.

## Cut before you deliver

Cut product-description headlines, the audience definition restated on the ad, facts the angle does
not need, disclaimers in the drafting voice, and lines a competitor could run unchanged. Never cut
below what each variation carries. Return the format the user asked for. Notes on sources or
assumptions follow the ads, briefly, only when needed.

## Platform facts (checked Oct 2026; confirm in the current specs before launch)

- **Meta (Facebook, Instagram):** feeds show about the first 125 characters of primary text before
  "See more". Headlines show about 40 characters; some mobile placements cut near 27. Descriptions
  are often hidden. One ad can carry up to 5 primary texts and 5 headlines that Meta mixes, so each
  must stand alone. Video: captions on, 4:5 for feed, 9:16 for Stories and Reels with text clear of
  the bottom and top bands.
- **Google responsive search:** up to 15 headlines of 30 characters, 4 descriptions of 90, and 2 paths
  of 15. Google shows up to 3 headlines and 2 descriptions in any order, so each asset must read
  alone and none may repeat another. Put the keyword in at least 2 headlines. Pin only what must
  always show.
- **LinkedIn sponsored content:** about 150 characters of intro text show before "see more" (600
  maximum); headlines show about 70 characters (200 maximum). The reader is at work: open on a work
  situation. Document ads and Lead Gen Forms suit give-first offers.
- **TikTok in-feed:** ad text up to 100 characters; Spark Ads use the post's caption. 9:16 video.
  The first 2 seconds decide the view: show the problem or the result on screen at once, and keep
  text inside the safe zone. A person speaking to camera reads as native; a brand logo opening does not.

## After drafting

Run [the check](anti-patterns.md): remove each claim the facts do not support and add nothing new.
Never launch, publish, spend, upload an audience or asset, or change a live account without the
user's approval for that exact action.
