# Annotated ad sets: Meta and TikTok

Every product, person, quote, rating, and number on this page is synthetic, invented to teach
decisions. No ad was run, and no result is reported. Each pair gives the facts, a strong set, a
weak set, and the decisions that separate them. Read for the decisions. The sets differ in length,
shape, and order on purpose; do not copy one as a template.

## Meta · shift scheduling for restaurants · leads on pain

Facts: Rotawise builds the weekly schedule and posts it to every employee's phone. Staff swap shifts
in the app; the manager approves with one tap. Median weekly scheduling time across 600 restaurants:
40 minutes, down from about 3 hours. Quote, permission given, from a general manager of two
taquerias: "Nobody texts me at 6 a.m. about swaps anymore." $49 a month per location, 14-day free
trial. Goal: trial starts.

```ad-meta
Product: Rotawise

### Variation 1 · pain
Primary text: Sunday night, and next week's schedule still lives in a group chat, a whiteboard photo, and your head.
Rotawise puts it on everyone's phone. Staff swap shifts in the app, and you approve with one tap.
Scheduling drops to 40 minutes a week for the median manager, down from about 3 hours.
$49 a month per location. Free for 14 days.
Headline: Post next week's schedule tonight
Visual: Screen recording: a manager drags three shifts into place and taps Publish; a row of phones on a prep counter lights up.

### Variation 2 · outcome
Primary text: Your day off, and your phone stays quiet.
When a cook needs a swap, they offer the shift in Rotawise, a teammate takes it, and you approve from wherever you are.
The median manager now spends 40 minutes a week on the schedule, down from about 3 hours.
Try it free for 14 days.
Headline: Approve swaps from anywhere
Visual: A manager at a market stall glances at a notification, "Swap: Dani to Marco, Sat dinner", taps Approve, and goes back to shopping.

### Variation 3 · proof
Primary text: "Nobody texts me at 6 a.m. about swaps anymore."
That's a general manager who runs two taquerias on Rotawise. Staff swap shifts in the app; the manager approves with one tap.
Free for 14 days, then $49 a month per location.
Headline: Try Rotawise free for 14 days
Visual: The quote in large type over a dim, empty kitchen at dawn; a phone on the counter shows one approved swap.
```

```text
Weak set (two of three shown)

1. Across 600 restaurants, the median Rotawise manager schedules a week in 40 minutes,
   down from 3 hours (Rotawise customer data, 2026). Rotawise is shift scheduling software
   for independent restaurants with swaps and one-tap approvals. $49/month. 14-day trial.
   Headline: Shift scheduling software for restaurants

2. Rotawise managers save hours. Across 600 restaurants, the median manager schedules a week
   in 40 minutes (Rotawise customer data, 2026). Results vary by restaurant.
   Headline: Save hours on scheduling every week
```

```text
Too thin (the pain variation cut to its angle)

Sunday night, and next week's schedule still lives in a group chat, a whiteboard photo, and your head.
Headline: Make Sunday nights shorter
```

**Why the strong set wins.** Each variation is a separate bet: the Sunday-night chore, the quiet
day off, and a peer's words. A manager who ignores one may stop for another. The weak set opens all
three on the same statistic, so it is one ad written three ways, and Meta would deliver it as one.

- The first lines name moments a manager has lived, and the product arrives in line two.
- A manager sees only one of these ads. So each one, whatever its angle, says what Rotawise does,
  gives a reason to believe, and states the free trial. The thin version keeps the pain line and
  drops the rest: the reader recognizes the problem and learns nothing to act on.
- The 40-minute figure supports two variations and leads none. Support may repeat across the set;
  the lead may not. It keeps "median", the one qualifier truth needs.
- "Customer data, 2026" and "results vary" are for the operator note and the facts check, not the
  feed. The strong set keeps the source in a note: median of 600 restaurants, from account records.
- "Shift scheduling software for restaurants" is a category, not a reason to click.

## Meta · piano learning app · leads on proof

Facts: Keystep teaches piano songs on the user's own keyboard. It listens through the phone's
microphone and waits at each note until the right key is played. Lessons are 10 minutes and resume
where the last one stopped. 4.7 stars
from 22,000 App Store ratings. Review, permission given: "Six weeks in, I played my daughter's
recital piece with her." 7-day free trial, then $12.99 a month or $79.99 a year. Audience: adults
who own a keyboard they do not play. Goal: trial starts.

```ad-meta
Product: Keystep

### Variation 1 · proof
Primary text: "Six weeks in, I played my daughter's recital piece with her."
That's a Keystep review. The app listens through your phone and waits at every note until you find it, so you learn songs, not scales.
7 days free, then $79.99 a year.
Headline: The piano app that waits for you
Visual: Two pairs of hands, one small and one adult, on one keyboard; the review line in handwritten type. Label the scene as representative in the production brief.

### Variation 2 · comparison
Primary text: Is the keyboard in the spare room holding up a stack of mail?
Prop your phone on the music stand, pick a song you already know, and play along. Keystep hears every note and waits until you find it.
Rated 4.7 from 22,000 App Store ratings. 7 days free.
Headline: Play the songs you actually like
Visual: A hand lifts mail off a dusty keyboard, props a phone on the stand, and plays the first bar of a song.

### Variation 3 · objection
Primary text: Lessons take 10 minutes and pick up where you stopped, so a tired Tuesday still counts.
Keystep listens as you play your own keyboard. Rated 4.7 from 22,000 App Store ratings.
Start free for 7 days, then $12.99 a month.
Headline: Start your free week
Visual: Phone timer at 10:00 on the music stand; a time-lapse of one short lesson; the timer ends as the song plays through.
```

```text
Weak set (one of three shown; the other two change only the first sentence)

Keystep is a piano learning app for adults who own a keyboard but don't play it.
It listens through your microphone and gives real-time feedback on every note.
Rated 4.7 from 22,000 ratings. Individual progress depends on practice time.
Headline: Piano lessons app for adults
```

**Why the strong set wins.** The review is better than any line the brand could write, so it leads
one variation word for word. The other two do not repeat it: one names the dusty keyboard the reader
already owns, the other removes the doubt about time. Both still say what the app does and carry
the rating and the free week as support, so a reader who sees only that ad can act on it.

The weak version restates the audience definition to the audience, describes features instead of
the moment, and adds a hedge that tells the reader it might not work. If a claim needs a hedge to be
true, choose a different claim.

## Meta · carbon-steel pan · leads on objection

Facts: Emberline is a carbon-steel frying pan that ships pre-seasoned, so eggs release on day one.
It has no coating to wear off and builds seasoning with use. Works on induction. $85, free US
shipping, 100-night trial with returns accepted after use. 4.8 stars from 3,100 reviews on the
brand's store. Audience: home cooks who keep replacing nonstick pans. Goal: purchases.

```ad-meta
Product: Emberline

### Variation 1 · objection
Primary text: Heard that carbon steel takes weeks of seasoning before an egg will release?
Emberline arrives seasoned. Crack an egg on day one and slide it straight onto the plate.
Rated 4.8 from 3,100 reviews. $85 with free US shipping.
Headline: Seasoned before it ships
Visual: One take from box to stove: pan out of the box, onto the burner, egg cracked, egg slid onto a plate with the spatula.

### Variation 2 · comparison
Primary text: Replacing the nonstick pan again?
Emberline is carbon steel, so there is no coating to scratch off, and it gets better the more you cook in it.
4.8 stars from 3,100 reviews. $85, with 100 nights to decide.
Headline: A pan with no coating to wear off
Visual: Split frame: a scratched nonstick pan goes into a donation box; the steel pan goes on a hot burner.

### Variation 3 · offer
Primary text: Cook with it for 100 nights. If it doesn't earn a spot on your stove, send it back, even used.
Emberline is carbon steel that arrives seasoned and works on induction.
Rated 4.8 from 3,100 reviews. $85, free US shipping.
Headline: 100-night trial, returns even if used
Visual: Quick cuts of one pan across many dinners (eggs, steak, cornbread) with a caption counting the nights.
```

```text
Weak set (all three shown, headlines only)

Carbon steel, not nonstick.
Seasoned, not sticky.
Lighter, not flimsy.
```

**Why the strong set wins.** For this buyer the main barrier is a belief: carbon steel sounds like
work. The set leads by answering that doubt, then tests the comparison with the pan they already
own, then lets the trial terms carry the third ad, because a 100-night trial with used returns is
news in itself. The rating and the price sit under all three, after the angle has done its work.

The weak set applies one headline formula to every variation. One contrast can work; three make the
set read as a template, and "lighter" is not in the facts at all.

## Meta · beginner strength training · leads on identity

Facts: Ironwalk is a strength-training app. Three full-body workouts a week, 35 minutes each. Every
lift has a 20-second demo and a starting weight chosen for someone who has never lifted. Rest timers
are built in. Quote, permission given, from a member: "First gym I didn't feel lost in." 14-day free
trial, then $14.99 a month. Audience: adults who want to start lifting and feel lost in a weight
room. Goal: trial starts.

```ad-meta
Product: Ironwalk

### Variation 1 · identity
Primary text: You walk into the weight room, look around, and end up on the treadmill again.
Ironwalk tells you which lift is next, how heavy to start, and shows a short demo before the first rep.
"First gym I didn't feel lost in," says one member. 14 days free, then $14.99 a month.
Headline: Know exactly what to lift today
Visual: Point of view from the gym doorway: racks, benches, then the phone showing today's first lift with its demo looping.

### Variation 2 · objection
Primary text: Three workouts a week, 35 minutes each, warm-up and rest timers included.
Ironwalk shows a demo before every lift, so you never stand between sets wondering what comes next.
"First gym I didn't feel lost in," says one member. Try it free for 14 days.
Headline: A full plan for your first lifts
Visual: A 35:00 timer in the corner over a time-lapse of one complete session.

### Variation 3 · proof
Primary text: "First gym I didn't feel lost in."
That's an Ironwalk member. Every lift in the plan comes with a demo and a starting weight. 14 days free.
Headline: Try 14 days free
Visual: The quote over a calm shot of a member checking the phone between sets, then loading a light barbell.
```

```text
Weak set (opening lines only)

1. Over 40 and out of shape? Ironwalk can help.
2. Struggling with your weight? Start lifting today.
3. Ironwalk is the #1 app for beginners.
```

**Why the strong set wins.** The identity variation describes a scene the reader recognizes:
standing in the doorway, then retreating to the treadmill. It never says what the reader is. The weak
openings assert age, body, and health, which Meta's personal-attributes policy rejects, and they
insult the person they want to recruit. "#1" has no source in the facts, so the facts check removes it.

The member's quote is the only proof the facts supply. It leads one variation and supports the other
two, because each reader sees one ad and each ad needs a reason to believe.

## TikTok · stain stick · leads on demonstration

Facts: Blotwell is a pocket-size stain stick for fresh coffee, wine, and grease on cotton and
polyester; no rinse needed. 3 sticks for $14. 4.6 stars from 9,400 reviews. Goal: purchases.

```ad-tiktok
Product: Blotwell

### Variation 1 · outcome
Opening: Close-up of a white shirt cuff with a fresh coffee spill; on-screen text "watch the cuff". A hand rubs the stick over it twice and the stain lifts.
Body: Pull back to show the person in a work shirt, stick going back into a jacket pocket. End card: "Blotwell · 4.6 stars from 9,400 reviews".
Ad text: Blotwell lifts fresh coffee off a white cuff before the meeting. No rinse. 3 sticks for $14.

### Variation 2 · pain
Opening: A creator in a parked car holds up a shirt with a red wine mark and says, "Wedding starts in 40 minutes."
Body: Same shot, two passes of the stick, the creator holds the shirt to the window light; the mark is gone. In their own words, they name Blotwell and its 4.6-star rating.
Ad text: For the stain you find when you're already late. Pocket-size, no rinse. 3 sticks for $14.

### Variation 3 · comparison
Opening: A hand pushes a spray bottle and a scrub brush to the back of a drawer, then slips a stick into a coat pocket. On-screen: "the one that comes with you".
Body: Three quick saves on the go: lunch grease on a sleeve, coffee on a tote, wine on a dinner shirt. On-screen: "4.6 stars, 9,400 reviews".
Ad text: Blotwell lives in your pocket, not under the sink. No rinse. 3 sticks for $14.
```

```text
Weak set (one shown; the other two swap the caption only)

Opening: Brand logo animates in. Creator: "Hey guys! Today I want to tell you about Blotwell,
the stain stick that changed my life."
Ad text: Blotwell is the revolutionary stain remover everyone is talking about! Get yours today
and say goodbye to stains forever! Shop now!
```

**Why the strong set wins.** On TikTok the first two seconds decide whether anyone reads the ad
text, so each variation starts with the stain already on screen. The three openings are different
bets: watching it work, the moment of panic, and replacing the spray bottle. Each still names
Blotwell, shows the rating, and gives the price, because a viewer sees one video. The creator speaks in
their own words; the brief scripts the angle and the facts, not the lines. The weak version spends
the opening on a logo and a greeting, claims a result no fact supports, and its "variations" change
only the caption, which tests nothing.

## TikTok · chore app for roommates · leads on pain

Facts: Tidyturn is a shared chore chart. Chores rotate every Sunday. Roommates can check off chores
from a text link without installing the app. Free for up to 4 people; $3 a month for 5 or more.
Goal: app installs.

```ad-tiktok
Product: Tidyturn

### Variation 1 · pain
Opening: The camera pans across a sink stacked with dishes; on-screen text "day 4 of nobody's turn". The creator, off camera: "We have a system. It's called waiting."
Body: Cut to the phone: Tidyturn shows whose turn it is this week, with a check box.
Ad text: The dish standoff ends when Tidyturn decides whose turn it is. Free for up to 4 roommates.

### Variation 2 · objection
Opening: Creator to camera, phone in hand: "My roommates will not download one more app."
Body: Screen recording of a text with a Tidyturn link; a roommate taps it and checks off "trash" without installing anything.
Ad text: Roommates check off chores from a text link, no install. Free for up to 4 people.

### Variation 3 · outcome
Opening: A clean kitchen at night, then the phone showing the week's chart with every row checked. On-screen: "it rotates every Sunday".
Body: Quick cuts of each roommate doing a different chore, the Tidyturn chart updating each time.
Ad text: Chores rotate every Sunday, so nobody keeps score. Free for up to 4; $3 a month for 5+.
```

```text
Weak set (all three openings)

1. "Do you struggle with keeping your shared apartment clean?"
2. "Do you struggle with roommates who don't do chores?"
3. "Do you struggle with chore arguments?"
```

**Why the strong set wins.** The weak openings are one question reworded three times, asked in a
voice no roommate uses. The strong set shows the dish pile, answers the first objection a person
raises before inviting anyone ("they won't download it"), and shows the calm week. Each still names
Tidyturn, shows the chart doing its job, and says it is free for up to four, because a roommate who
sees only one video needs to know what to install and what it costs.
