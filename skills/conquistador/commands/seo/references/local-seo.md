# Local SEO

Use this method when a business wants to be found for a service in a place: a storefront, a
service-area business, a multi-location business, or a clinic. Keep diagnostic and audit runs
read-only. Changes to the website or to a business profile require separate approval.

## Record the inputs

Record the service, the target city or area, the business name, and the business type. Mark each
input that was not supplied as unknown.

## Classify the problem first

Look at what the local results for the query actually show: a map pack or business profiles, city or
service pages, directories, homepages, or articles. Classify the problem as one of:

- organic page ranking;
- map pack or business profile;
- local prominence;
- a mix of these.

Do not apply page edits to a map-pack problem.

## Check the page

- **Target.** The page clearly targets one service plus one city or area. Check the title, H1, intro,
  headings, body, image alt text, and internal anchors for the place. Add it only where it reads
  naturally.
- **Local proof.** List each item as present, missing, or not applicable: local project examples,
  areas served, local reviews, local photos, address, a tappable phone number, hours, licenses, and a
  map or directions.
- **Structured data.** LocalBusiness (or Organization) markup matches the visible name, address,
  phone, and hours. Treat a mismatch as a defect, not only as missing markup. Use
  [schema reference](schema-reference.md).

## Compare the business profile with the website

Compare the business profile (for example, Google Business Profile) with the website, field by field:
name, phone, address or service area, website URL, hours, primary category, secondary categories, and
services. Check that name, address, and phone match across the homepage, footer, contact page, and
structured data.

Name the services listed in the profile that have no page, and the pages for services that the
profile does not list.

If no profile URL is supplied, find the most likely listing from the domain, phone, and address, and
state your match confidence. Never invent profile data; mark fields you cannot see as unknown.

## Write the fixes

Write each fix as one specific action, for example "add a service page for X, which the profile
lists" or "change the footer phone number to match the profile". Never write "improve the profile".

When several pages compete for the same service and place, run the cannibalization procedure in
[technical audit](technical-audit.md).

When the page, contact details, structured data, categories, and local relevance are all in order,
say so. Then route the problem to factors outside the page: reviews, citations, links, proximity, or
local prominence. Do not add more on-page edits.

## Deliver

Return the top one to three issues, each with a priority of Critical, High, Medium, Low, or Unknown,
the evidence, and the specific fix. Group small issues together.
