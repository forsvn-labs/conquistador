# Headline patterns

Use these for a landing page, product page, or offer page headline. Start from the reader's want,
pain, or identity, in their words, not from the product. Short-form work does not use this file; it
follows the [short-form mode](../ENTRY.md). In long-form work, pick the proof and mechanism first
([sales argument](sales-argument.md)), then draft 5–10 headlines from different patterns and keep
the one that passes the tests below. Examples use invented products.

| Pattern | Example | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **The want, plainly** | Get your Sunday evenings back | The reader can picture the after-state |
| **The pain, stopped** | Stop rebuilding the rota every time someone swaps a shift | The old way hurts in a specific, repeated moment |
| **Without the usual cost** | Get paid without chasing | The usual way to get the outcome is what they hate |
| **Identity** | The sleep mask for people who hate sleep masks | The reader defines themselves against the category |
| **Outcome in a time frame** | Finish the quarterly access review in 3 days, not 19 | The proof supports the time for readers like them |
| **Their moment** | Sleep through the 5 a.m. sunrise | One scene sums up the problem |
| **Short parallel lines** | Fewer reminders. More done. | Two or three benefits carry equal weight |
| **The one different thing** | Chores they actually do, because Friday pays | Crowded market; the mechanism is the news |
| **Never again** | Never lose track of a repair again | The mechanism truly prevents it |
| **Objection turned around** | Why a $49 rota tool costs less than your free spreadsheet | You can show the arithmetic |
| **Tried that** | If shift apps did not stop the swaps, the rules were the problem | Solution-aware readers burned before |
| **Offer first** | Sleep in it for 60 nights. Then decide. | Warm traffic, or a risk reversal that removes the main fear |
| **Progress** | You ran week one. Eight to go. | The reader is already partway in, as on a paywall or upgrade |
| **Question in their words** | Still building the rota on Sunday night? | Only when every target reader answers yes |

A customer's result leads only when the reader knows the customer. Otherwise it supports from the
subhead. When a visitor must first learn what the product is, put the category in a small line above
the headline, not in the headline.

## Test each headline

- It starts from the reader's want, pain, or identity. If it could sit in a product directory as the
  listing, rewrite it.
- A busy reader understands it at a glance. Plain words beat clever ones.
- It promises a reward for reading on, and the next lines pay it off.
- It has a concrete detail: a number, a time, a picture. If a competitor could run it unchanged, add
  the detail only you can claim.
- It promises no more than the proof supports, and carries no source text or hedge.

The headline carries the promise. The subhead carries the mechanism and one proof point. Read the
headline and subhead together, then read the first screen alone.
