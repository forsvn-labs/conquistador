# Long-form section library

Use this for long-form pages over about 150 words: sales pages, landing pages, and offer pages.
Pick sections by the job they do in the [sales argument](sales-argument.md). Cut any section that
does not move the reader to the next decision.

| Section | Its job | Write it as |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Stop the right reader and state the promise | A headline from the reader's want or pain; a subhead with the mechanism and one proof point; one action and what happens next |
| Proof strip | Earn the next scroll | One specific result, permitted logos, or a count |
| Problem | Make the reader say "that is my week" | A concrete scene in the customer's words, then the cost of leaving it |
| Mechanism | Make the promise believable | The cause of the problem, then the one thing the product does differently |
| How it works | Make it feel doable | Three steps; the last step is the first useful result, not a setup task |
| Proof block | Back the main claim | A named before-and-after in the customer's own words |
| Objections | Remove the reasons to wait | Setup time, integrations, data handling, switching, price, each answered with a fact |
| Offer | Make the deal clear | What is included, price, period, risk reversal, next step |
| Final call to action | Close | Restate the outcome and the offer; one button |

## Page structures by type

- **SaaS trial page:** hero, proof strip, problem and mechanism, how it works, proof block,
  objections, pricing, final call to action.
- **Demo-led B2B page:** hero with a "see it on your data" call to action, problem, mechanism,
  role-by-role outcomes, security and integrations, case study, demo call to action that says who
  joins the call and for how long.
- **Direct-response offer page:** the full nine-step order in the sales argument; longer copy is
  fine when every section answers a real question.
- **Launch page:** what changed and what it means for the reader, a short demo, early proof if it
  exists, the launch terms with their real end date, one action.
- **Units under about 150 words:** use the [short-form mode](../ENTRY.md) instead of this
  library.

A list of features with a button at the end is not an argument. Connect each feature to the
outcome it produces ("which means…").

## Choosing testimonials

Prefer a named person, a before-and-after in their own words, and a detail only a real customer
would know. Place each testimonial next to the claim it supports. "Great product" proves nothing;
leave it out.
