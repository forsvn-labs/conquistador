# Write a lead magnet page

Use when the copy offers a guide, template, checklist, calculator, or other resource in exchange for
an email address or another action. A lead magnet page is short-form: draft it in one pass with the
[short-form checklist](short-form-copy.md). The reader wants the result the resource gives, not the
file.

- **Headline:** the problem the resource solves or the result it gives, in the reader's words, with
  the most concrete specific the facts hold. "No repair left open past 14 days without you knowing"
  beats "Track repairs with our free spreadsheet".
- **Subhead:** what it is, that it is free, and the one thing that makes it worth an email, in one or
  two sentences.
- **Bullets:** three or four, one per thing inside, each with what the reader gets from it. A label,
  then the benefit, scans well: "Year-end summary: one repair total for your accountant". A bare label
  ("Year-end summary tab") is not enough. A short video or setup note can be the last bullet.
- **Button:** name the thing they get: "Get the free Repair Log by email".

Leave the maker's company out of the hero unless the reader knows it. Do not restate the audience.

Then check the exchange. Describe only contents that exist; if the resource is not built yet, say
so to the owner. If delivery needs an email address, the form makes that plain. Do not imply
instant delivery if fulfillment is manual. Do not promise a saving or result the facts do not
support; a benefit that follows directly from the contents is fair.

Return the copy, then an operator note with the destination, fulfillment terms, and anything to
confirm. Sending, collecting contacts, and publishing remain separate authorized actions.
