# Check the finished copy against the facts

Run this after a sales page or sales letter is drafted from the [sales argument](sales-argument.md).
All other copy gets only the facts check in the [copy entry](../ENTRY.md). This review checks what
the copy asks the reader to believe and do. It removes unsupported claims and nothing else. It does
not choose the voice, flatten the headline, or
blur a specific claim that has a source. It does not add disclaimers, hedges, or source text to the
page.

## Build the claim record

List each statement that could change the reader's decision. The record is the operator note; it
does not go on the page.

| Location | Claim as the reader understands it | Source | Scope (who, when, how many) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero subhead | "Northpier went from 6 hours of rota work a week to 40 minutes" | Practice manager interview | One clinic, four weeks | Keep |

Actions are: keep, narrow, remove, or ask the owner. Never invent a testimonial, number, customer, or
deadline to fill a gap. A quote stays a quote only when the source supports the words and permits
their use.

## Unsupported claim or fair inference

Remove or narrow an unsupported claim: a result, number, customer, guarantee, or capability the
facts do not establish, or a single result widened into a general one. Keep a fair inference: a
benefit that follows directly from a stated fact. If staff swap shifts inside the app, "no more
chasing swaps by text" is a fair inference; keep it. "Every clinic cuts rota work to 40 minutes" from
one clinic's result is unsupported; narrow it to that clinic. When in doubt, ask whether a reader
who knew every fact would call the line false. If not, it stays.

## Keep a sourced specific exact

Keep a sourced specific exact. If a claim goes beyond its evidence, narrow it and keep the number:
name the customer, or add a light qualifier the reader needs ("across 31 teams"). Do not swap a
sourced number for vaguer language ("hours, not days") to feel safe. The source, period, and scope
go in the operator note.

## Read the reader's path

Read the page in order. Check that the reader can tell what the offer is, what backs each claim,
and what the next step commits them to. A button that says "Download" must not start a sales
process. Price, renewal, cancellation, and delivery terms must match the owner's terms. Do not
invent a guarantee.

## Return the verdict

Return PASS when no unsupported claim or offer mismatch remains. Return REVISE with the location,
the likely misunderstanding, the evidence needed, and the smallest fix. Fix the unit, not the whole
page. Do not REVISE a line for being confident, vivid, or selective. A PASS is an editorial
verdict; it is not approval to publish and does not predict conversion.

For a costly commitment or regulated claim, name the human reviewer and the exact open statement.
More model opinions cannot replace missing evidence or human authority.
