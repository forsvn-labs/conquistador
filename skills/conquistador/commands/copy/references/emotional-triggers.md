# Use audience context responsibly

Use when tone depends on a consequential situation, such as a frustrating task or an important purchase. Start with the user's supplied audience research and voice examples.

Distinguish a reported feeling from an inferred one. Describe an observable situation when the brief does not support a claim about emotion. Avoid asserting that all readers are afraid, ashamed, ambitious, or part of a particular group.

Choose language that helps the reader understand the offer. A difficult situation may warrant calm explanation and clear terms. An announcement may warrant enthusiasm about a verified capability. Intensity is not a quality target.

Check whether the draft pressures the reader through false scarcity, social exclusion, or an invented threat. Remove that pressure. State genuine deadlines or limited capacity only when the offer owner supplied them, including the relevant conditions.

When copy names a real risk, put a specific action the reader can take, and what that action changes, in the same unit. If no such action exists, remove the risk from the opening. Do not raise a risk's severity above the supplied evidence to make the action feel urgent.

Return the revised copy with any assumption about tone. Preserve facts and the reader's ability to decline. Do not score emotional density or claim that a trigger count predicts engagement.

Synthetic example: a backup product can explain how restoration works and what the user must configure. It should not invent a story about imminent data loss to frighten the reader into purchase.
