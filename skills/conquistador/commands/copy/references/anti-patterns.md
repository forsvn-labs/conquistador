# Copy failures to catch before delivery

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Headline describes the product, its category, or its features | Start from the reader's want, pain, or identity; move the product into the subhead |
| An unknown customer's result as the headline | Lead with the reader's outcome; put the result in the subhead |
| Every fact in the brief used in the hero | Keep one proof, one mechanism, and two or three benefits; move the rest down or out |
| Offer terms cut while selecting (price, trial, free, waitlist) | Put them back in the subhead, a bullet, or under the button |
| A vague headline when the facts hold a number, time, or result | Put the most concrete specific in the headline |
| Headline set options on the same angle | Give each its own: outcome, pain, proof, objection, identity |
| The audience definition restated on the page | Cut it, unless it filters out visitors the product cannot serve |
| Source, period, or survey text on the page | Move it to the operator note; keep a light qualifier only if the reader needs it |
| Hedges or disclaimers ("results may vary", "not a promise") | Cut them; remove the claim instead if it is unsupported |
| A sourced number blurred into "hours, not days" | Restore the exact number |
| Feature bullets with no benefit | Give each bullet what the reader gets; "Label: benefit" is fine |
| A promise bigger than the proof | Shrink the promise or add the proof; never stretch the proof |
| No mechanism, or a label posing as one | Say what the product does differently and why that changes the result |
| Product described, offer missing | State what they get, terms, risk reversal, and the next step |
| Invented quote, number, customer, or deadline | Remove it; ask the owner for real evidence |
| Button promises one thing, destination does another | Make the label match the real next step |

Editorial preference does not predict conversion. A PASS does not grant permission to publish.
