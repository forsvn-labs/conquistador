# Short-form copy: headlines, hero sections, lead magnet pages, and app pages

This checklist is the whole method for a headline or headline set, a hero section, a lead magnet
page, an app or consumer hero, and other copy under about 150 words. Write in one pass. Do not run
the review roles or load the long-form files. Longer pages, sales arguments, hero rewrites, and B2B
heroes that have to argue use the [complete method](../COMMAND.md).

## Headlines and headline sets

- **Put the most concrete specific in the headline** when the facts hold one: a number, a time, or
  a result. Start from the reader's want, pain, or identity; the specific makes it real. An unknown
  customer's result goes in the subhead.
- **Give each option in a set its own angle:** outcome, pain, proof, objection, or identity. Two
  options on one angle count as one. Put the offer terms in at least one subhead.

## Hero sections, lead magnet pages, and app pages

- **Shape:** a headline, a subheadline of one or two sentences, three or four bullets, and a button.
- **Write bullets that scan,** one idea each. A label, then the benefit, is fine: "Daily plan: 20
  minutes, picked for the rooms that need it".
- **Keep the offer terms a buyer needs to act:** price, trial length, free, beta or waitlist status,
  and what happens after the click. Selecting never cuts these. When price is the reason to switch,
  show both prices.
- **Keep one piece of social proof:** the strongest rating, count, or quote, exact.
- **Name the next step on the button:** "Start my 7-day free trial".
- **Cut adjectives, not facts.** To shorten, cut adjectives, warm-up, and restated audience first.

## Check after the draft

One facts pass in the same context: remove claims the facts do not support, keep each fair
inference, and add nothing. Do not add disclaimers, hedges, or source text to the page. Confirm the
headline specific, the offer terms, and the proof survived. A number that needs its source gets a
short operator note after the copy. Publishing needs the owner's approval.

## Example: app hero

Both examples use invented products and numbers.

- **Tidyhour:** an iOS app that plans a 20-minute clean each day and rotates rooms, so each room gets
  done once a week. Audience: people who only clean in a rush before guests arrive. 4.8 from 3,400
  App Store ratings. Offer terms: `7-day free trial`, `$4.99 a month`.

**Strong** (check: landing; leads with: outcome; form: short; product: Tidyhour)

> Keep your home guest-ready in 20 minutes a day
>
> Tidyhour plans one 20-minute clean a day, room by room, so the whole home gets done every week. Try it free for 7 days, then $4.99 a month.
>
> - Daily plan: 20 minutes, picked for the rooms that need it
> - Every room, every week: no more weekend blitz
> - Rated 4.8 from 3,400 App Store ratings
>
> Start my 7-day free trial

## Example: product page headline set

- **Edgekeep:** a 21 cm chef's knife, $89, with free sharpening by mail for life, back within 7 days.
  Audience: home cooks whose knives have gone dull. 4.7 from 2,800 reviews. Offer terms: `$89`,
  `free sharpening`.

**Strong** (check: web; leads with: set; form: short; product: Edgekeep; angles: outcome, pain, proof, objection, identity)

> 1. Sharp on day one. Sharp in year ten.
>    Free sharpening by mail for life, back within 7 days.
> 2. Stop sawing at tomatoes
>    Mail it in whenever it dulls; it comes back sharp within a week.
> 3. 4.7 stars from 2,800 reviews
>    For a chef's knife you never have to sharpen yourself.
> 4. Never pay for sharpening again
>    $89 once, with free sharpening by mail for life.
> 5. You cook every night. Your knife should keep up.
>    Send it in when it dulls; it's back sharp within 7 days.
