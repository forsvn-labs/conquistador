# Describe an observed product change

Use when the brief contains a real account of a product decision, customer experience, or implementation change that helps the buyer evaluate the offer.

Collect the permitted record, participants, dates or sequence, change made, and observed result. Distinguish what happened from why someone thinks it happened. If the account is incomplete, write only the supported portion or flag the missing detail.

Select details relevant to the reader's decision. Explain the starting situation, the actual change, and its limits in an order that makes the account easy to follow. There is no mandatory dramatic arc, failure scene, revelation, or transformation.

Preserve quotations exactly when their source and use are authorized. Otherwise paraphrase accurately and identify the source in the draft notes. Never construct a customer voice from several unrelated accounts.

Connect the account to the current offer only where the product and conditions match. A historical result does not promise the same outcome to every reader. Include qualifiers near the result.

Return the story block with a fact record and unresolved permissions. Use [copy review](copy-review.md) before delivery. When no real story is supplied, use a plain capability explanation instead.

Synthetic example: a team changed its export layout after a permitted usability session. Describe the change and what participants could complete. Do not invent an origin anecdote or claim company-wide productivity gains.
