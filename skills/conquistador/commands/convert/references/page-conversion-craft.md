# Page conversion craft: diagnose and rewrite a landing or signup page

Use this to improve conversion on one landing, signup, pricing, or offer page. Read the page as a
visitor, rewrite it from the persuasion craft, then pick one test. The `copy` command owns the full
craft; the parts a rewrite needs most are below.

## Read the page as a visitor

Answer these in order. The first "no" is usually where the page loses people.

1. **Match:** Does the first screen repeat the words and offer of the ad, email, or search that
   brought them? A visitor who clicked "rota template" and lands on "workforce platform" leaves.
2. **Clarity:** In five seconds, can a visitor say what they get and why it matters to them?
3. **Value:** Is the outcome concrete, and is the mechanism believable?
4. **Proof:** Is the strongest proof next to the claim it supports, in the first screen when it is
   the best argument?
5. **Anxiety:** At the button, are the real fears answered: price, card, data, cancelling, setup
   time, and "will sales call me"?
6. **Friction:** What does the next step cost: fields, steps, a password, load time on a phone?
7. **Distraction:** What competes with the main action: navigation, a second offer, exit links?

Match and clarity failures map to Attention and Comprehension on the break ladder in `COMMAND.md`;
value and proof to Belief and Motivation; anxiety, friction, and distraction to Friction.

## Rewrite the first screen first

- **Headline:** the visitor's want, pain, or identity, in their words, sized to the proof. A
  product description or an unknown customer's result is not a headline.
- **Subhead:** the mechanism and one proof point. Keep a sourced number exact; its source, period,
  and scope go in the operator note. Keep only the qualifier a reader needs ("across 31 teams").
- **Bullets:** two or three benefits, not features. Leave the other facts lower on the page.
- **Call to action:** a verb and what they get. Say what happens after the click.
- **Line under the button:** the two or three fears from the anxiety question, answered with the
  offer's real terms.

Do not restate the audience unless it filters out visitors the product cannot serve.

Then move proof next to its claims, put objection answers beside the action they block, and cut
sections that do not move the visitor to a decision.

## Forms and signup

Ask only for what the next step needs now. Move qualification questions after signup or into the
onboarding call, unless sales needs them to route the lead; then test their removal against lead
quality as a guardrail. Offer sign-in with an existing work account when the product supports it.
Show what happens after submitting. Validate fields as the visitor types, keep their input after an
error, and use the right phone keyboard for each field. When the product allows it, let the visitor
complete the first useful action before asking for an account.

## Message match by traffic source

- **Cold social traffic:** usually problem-aware. Lead with the problem and offer a lower-commitment
  first step.
- **Search:** mirror the query in the headline; the visitor is checking that they found it.
- **Email to the list:** lead with the offer and its terms.
- **Partner or referral:** name the context that sent them.

Build a separate page for a source whose awareness differs sharply from the rest.

## Collect evidence before choosing a cause

Watch session recordings of visitors who reached the form and did not submit. Note rage clicks,
taps on things that are not links, scrolling back and forth between sections, and long pauses on
the price. Starting point (Oct 2026): review 20–30 such sessions, then replace the number with what
your traffic allows. Pair recordings with one on-page question at the moment of hesitation.

## Check whether a test can reach a result

Estimate the visitors each variant needs before you run a test. A common approximation for two
variants, at 95% confidence and 80% power, is this formula: visitors per variant ≈ 16 × p × (1 − p)
÷ d², where p is the current conversion rate and d is the smallest absolute change worth detecting.
Synthetic example: p = 0.04 and d = 0.01 gives 16 × 0.04 × 0.96 ÷ 0.0001 ≈ 6,100 visitors per variant.
If the page cannot reach that in the observation window, test a bigger change, use a higher-traffic
step, or ship the fix on qualitative evidence and watch one guardrail.

## After the rewrite

Check every claim against its source and keep sourced specifics exact. Remove invented proof and
unsupported claims; keep fair inferences that follow directly from the facts. Do not add hedges or
disclaimers. Give the sources in a short operator note after the page.
Deploying the page or starting the test needs the owner's approval.
