# Cold email and DM craft

Write the message a busy stranger reads in seconds and answers in one line. Draft from this file,
then run the fact check in [the method](../method.md). Email format and inbox basics are in
[email](../channels/email.md); follow-ups are in [sequences](sequences.md); annotated pairs are in
[first touches](../examples/pairs-first-touch.md) and [relationships](../examples/pairs-relationships.md).

## Principles

1. **Relevance beats personalization.** A reason for this person, now, earns the read. A compliment
   or a fact copied from a profile does not. With no real reason, name the group you write to.
2. **Lead with the strongest true fact.** The subject and first line carry what the reader cares
   about most: their situation, a peer's result, or the sender's best credential.
3. **Cut what is not fact, offer, or ask.** No pleasantries, no name-and-title introduction, no
   industry background, no feature list, no stacked hedges.
4. **One idea, one ask.** A message argues one thing and asks one question.
5. **Make the offer concrete.** Say what the reader gets, how soon, and what starting costs them.
   Add a guarantee only when the sender supplies its terms.
6. **Sound like one person.** Use "I", plain words, contractions, and short lines. Match the reader's
   register. Sign with a first name.
7. **Each touch earns its place.** A follow-up brings a new reason or it is not sent.

## Choose a structure by situation

| Situation | Order of the body | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Strong signal: a dated, sourced event about this person or account | Signal → what it often means, as a question or "if" → one proof or offer line | Interest or current practice |
| No signal: you know the segment only | Peer result, or the costly moment as a question → how, in one line | Interest |
| Credential-led: the reader judges the sender (investor, partner, senior hire) | Three plain facts: origin, traction, what you want | Specific, time-boxed call |
| Give-first: you can show real work now | The finding or sample → why it matters → offer the rest | "Want the rest?" |
| Warm: a connector, a past talk, or an action they expect you to know | The connection → why now | Specific call or reply |
| Follow-up or breakup | See [sequences](sequences.md) | A changed or smaller ask |

Rank signals before you pick: something the person said or did beats a company event (hire,
launch, expansion, funding); a role or company size is segment, not signal. Use one signal only.
At scale, the signal line is a merge field fed by a sourced column, one source and date per row.
Rows without a source get the no-signal version. Never leave a free placeholder to fill by guess.

## Openers

Open with the reader's world or the strongest fact:

- the costly moment as a question: "Who checks carrier invoices at {{company}} today?"
- the signal, plainly: "You posted a freight audit role this month."
- a peer result: "`{{peer}}` went from `{{before}}` to `{{after}}` on `{{metric}}`."
- the credential, as a fact: "`{{origin}}`, now building `{{product}}`."

Cut these openers: hope-you're-well lines, "My name is… and I'm the…", "I came across your
profile", "I wanted to reach out", and trend sentences about the industry.

## Subject lines

- **Fact pair:** the two strongest facts, joined by a bar: `{{credential}} | {{traction}}`.
- **Reader's topic:** a noun from their world: `carrier invoices at {{company}}`.
- **Signal:** `your {{event}}`, when the first line names it.
- **Connector:** `{{connector}} suggested I write`, only when true.

Write in plain case. The subject must match the first line. No buzzwords, teasers, emoji, or
"Re:" on a new message.

## Asks

| Ask | Use when | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Interest | Cold, no relationship | "Worth a look?" |
| Current practice | You want a conversation, not a meeting | "How do you handle this today?" |
| Give-first | The work is ready to send | "Want the other two?" |
| Specific call | Calls are the reader's job, or they showed interest | "Open to a 20-minute call next week?" |
| Referral | Late in a sequence only | "Who owns this at {{company}}?" |

No calendar link and no second question in a cold first touch.

## Starting points (Oct 2026)

Replace these with the sender's own reply data once it exists.

| Item | Starting point |
|---|---|
| First-touch body | 50–90 words, three to five sentences |
| Follow-up body | Under 70 words; breakup under 45 |
| Sentence | Under 20 words; vary the length |
| Paragraph | One or two sentences |
| Links in a first touch | None |
| Subject | Two to six words |
| Test | One variable per test (angle, offer, proof, or ask); decide on positive replies after about 300 delivered per variant |

Word counts exclude the greeting, sign-off, and footer. Test angles, not adjectives: a synonym
swap teaches nothing.
