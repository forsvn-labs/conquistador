# Selection evidence

Record source, date, access class (public, operator-supplied, permissioned), exact observation,
recipient match, why it matters to this offer, the limited inference used in the draft, and an
expiry date. Set the expiry when you capture the signal. Do not open with an expired signal, even
if it may still be true; ask whether the situation is current instead. Keep an internal source
excerpt only as long as needed to verify the statement. Do not reproduce private or sensitive
details in copy.

A public product change may justify asking about that change; it does not prove workload, budget,
or dissatisfaction. A team announcement does not identify who owns a purchase. A job title and
company category explain a segment but do not establish an individual event.

If two sources disagree, mark the conflict and avoid the disputed assertion. If a source is old,
ask whether the situation is still current rather than silently updating it. If a URL cannot be
read, say it was not verified; do not treat the URL itself as evidence of its alleged content.

Targeted research needs a real signal. Missing signal yields a generic NEEDS_SIGNAL template.
Commercial drafting may use a conditional use case with its targeting uncertainty disclosed.
Neither route allows a fabricated observed-person opener or enrichment without authorization.

## Gate the account before drafting

Before research, enrichment, or drafting, check the account against the exclusion list: existing
customer, open opportunity, direct competitor, already contacted in this campaign, and suppressed
contact. An excluded account never enters cold outreach. When the operator has not supplied CRM
state, list the check as a pre-send gap.

Draft for an account only when its record answers five questions: why it fits, who the buyer is,
what changed recently, which claims the sender can support, and what the next action is. If an
answer is unknown, return the account to monitoring instead of filling the gap with copy.

## Keep the list clean

List collection is an operator action that needs approval; it is not a drafting step. Obey site
terms and access controls. Do not bypass paywalls, logins, or rate limits.

- Prefer sources where people are listed so they can be found for that work, such as license
  lookups, association directories, and exhibitor or speaker lists. Record the source, pull date,
  and listing or license status.
- Remove duplicates across sources before you count the list. Match on name, location, business,
  and email domain together.
- Verify each address before the first send and record the verification date.
- Normalize merge fields: take the first token of a name field, drop legal suffixes from company
  names, and reject rows whose name field holds a brand or channel name. Do not wrap variables in
  quotes or formatting.
- Cut a personalization line that reads like a pasted profile summary instead of a note from one
  person to another.
