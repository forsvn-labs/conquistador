# Define the next decision

Write down what a reply or click commits the recipient to. Then make the wording match it.
A request for a meeting must not look like a request for a document. A document offer must name an
existing document or disclose that preparation is proposed. Avoid links whose destination or
tracking behavior the sender has not reviewed.

Choose one next decision. If two answers are prerequisites, ask the necessary one first instead
of stacking questions. In research, ask about current behavior in an open form; do not ask the
recipient to endorse the sender's diagnosis. In a reply, the next decision may be to end contact.

State time, cost, access, or data requirements when known and material. Mark unknown requirements
for sender review. Do not promise no obligation if answering enrolls the recipient into a sequence.

A refusal must work. Do not make an opt-out conditional on a reason. Silence ends a single bounded
research attempt. A commercial sequence uses explicit sender-set cadence and suppression rules;
the draft does not authorize any scheduled touch.
