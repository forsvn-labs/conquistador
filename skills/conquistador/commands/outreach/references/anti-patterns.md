# Outreach failures and repairs

| Failure | Why it fails | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| “I saw” without an inspected source | Invents an observation | Use verified evidence or a conditional use case |
| Public fact used to imply private distress | Inference exceeds evidence | Ask neutrally or omit the inference |
| Generic title praised as personal research | Misrepresents selection | Disclose segment-level relevance |
| Outcome asserted from a capability | Claims an unobserved result | Describe actual scope and limits |
| Research used as an undisclosed sales opener | Hides purpose | State commercial intent or remove the pitch |
| Fake reply subject or prior-conversation claim | Invents relationship | Use an accurate new-message subject |
| A free deliverable that does not exist | Misrepresents readiness | Say it is proposed work and state requirements |
| Multiple questions plus a calendar request | Leaves the decision unclear | Choose one next decision |
| Threat, guilt, or invented deadline | Undermines voluntary choice | State real constraints without pressure |
| More contact after a refusal | Ignores recipient control | End the sequence; propose required suppression |
| Passing review after cycles are exhausted | Hides unresolved failure | Return failure and the missing evidence |
| Protecting an earlier unsupported number during editing | Preserves error | Recheck against evidence and remove or narrow |

Do not ban ordinary pronouns, greetings, punctuation, or formal language as a substitute for review.
Do not assume short copy is useful or long copy is wrong. Check whether each part supports the
recipient's decision, and trim material that does not. Keep legal and platform checks explicit but
unverified unless the operator supplies applicable requirements and evidence.
