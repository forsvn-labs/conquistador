# Outreach failures and repairs

## Craft failures

| Failure | Why it fails | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Opens with the sender's name and title | Spends the preview on the least interesting fact | Open with the reader's situation, the signal, or the strongest fact |
| Pleasantries or "I wanted to reach out" | Delays the point | Delete; start with the fact |
| Industry background or a trend sentence | Generic; the reader knows their industry | Delete, or replace with one fact about this reader |
| Buzzword or teaser subject | Reads as marketing; mismatches the body | Use the fact pair or the reader's topic |
| Feature list | Asks the reader to do the matching | Name the one outcome that fits this reader |
| Stacked hedges ("just", "maybe", "I think") | Sounds unsure of the claim | State the supported fact plainly, or cut it |
| Two asks, or a question plus a calendar link | Leaves the decision unclear | Choose one next decision |
| "Just following up" or a recap | Adds nothing new | Bring a new element or stop |
| A profile summary pasted as personalization | Reads as automated | Use one sourced signal, or the no-signal structure |

## Honesty failures

| Failure | Why it fails | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| "I saw" without an inspected source | Invents an observation | Use verified evidence or a conditional use case |
| Public fact used to imply private distress | Inference exceeds evidence | Ask neutrally or omit the inference |
| Outcome asserted from a capability | Claims an unobserved result | Describe actual scope and limits |
| Research used as an undisclosed sales opener | Hides purpose | State commercial intent or remove the pitch |
| Fake reply subject or prior-conversation claim | Invents a relationship | Use an accurate new-message subject |
| A free deliverable that does not exist | Misrepresents readiness | Say it is proposed work and state requirements |
| Threat, guilt, or invented deadline | Undermines voluntary choice | State real constraints without pressure |
| More contact after a refusal | Ignores recipient control | End the sequence; propose required suppression |
| Passing review after cycles are exhausted | Hides an unresolved failure | Return the failure and the missing evidence |
| Keeping an earlier unsupported number while editing | Preserves an error | Recheck against evidence; remove or narrow |

Do not ban ordinary pronouns, greetings, punctuation, or formal language as a substitute for review.
Short is a starting point, not a rule: keep a needed qualification even when it costs words. Keep
legal and platform checks explicit but unverified unless the operator supplies the requirements.
