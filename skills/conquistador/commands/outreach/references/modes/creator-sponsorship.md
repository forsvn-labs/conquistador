# creator-sponsorship

Use this mode when the sender pays a creator with an existing audience to show the product in their
own content. Confirm first that the niche fits the channel with
[creator channel fit](../../../channels/references/creator-channel-fit.md). The brief belongs to the
[UGC pack](../../../campaign/references/platform-intelligence/ugc.md), and running the program belongs
to the [creator program model](../../../campaign/references/distribution-models/creator-program.md).

## Vet the creator before you write

Make each check quick. When a creator needs long deliberation, move on.

1. **Typical views.** Use the typical views per recent post and the floor below which views rarely
   fall. Ignore follower count and one-off hits. Record the window and the source.
2. **Comments.** Look for questions, stories, and replies between commenters. A post with many views
   and almost no comments is a weak sign.
3. **Presence.** The creator talks to camera, so viewers feel they know the person.
4. **Audience intent.** The audience watches for a reason that matches the product, not only the same
   topic. People who watch food content for fun are not a meal-tracking audience. You buy access to
   the audience, not to the creator.

Reject a creator whose engagement looks purchased or pooled.

## Prepare before the first message

Make the brand profile credible before outreach: a clear bio, pinned real product demos, and
disclosed collaboration posts with past creators. Never buy followers or engagement to make it look
larger.

To find candidates, use an account whose feed follows what the ideal customer watches, or give a
researcher explicit criteria: view range, view floor, comment quality, audience intent, and topics.

## Write the first message

Keep it to three parts and no feature list:

1. Say that this is a paid sponsorship in the first words.
2. Describe the product in one sentence.
3. Ask whether they are interested.

## Move to a call

Move to a call early. Discuss how the product fits their existing content before you discuss price,
and ask for the creator's rate first. Price every deal on a conservative estimate of views across the
agreed posts, never on follower count.

## Choose the pay structure

Write the trade-off for the operator; do not apply one structure as universal.

| Structure | Use when | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat fee per agreed set of posts | You want the full upside of a post that overperforms, and the creator's view floor is stable | You carry the risk of a weak post; add a minimum-view guarantee set below the creator's normal floor, and pay part up front and the rest on delivery |
| Base fee plus capped per-view bonus | The creator rejects the flat fee, or views vary too much to price | You pay more for a hit, up to the cap; state the cap per post and the fixed window in which views count |

Under a per-view structure, an overperforming post costs more, so the deal behaves more like bought
media. Record the chosen structure, the reason, the view source, and the counting window in the
contract.

Prefer multi-post or recurring agreements over one-off posts. Make disclosure of the paid relationship
a term of every offer.

## Handle common objections

- "A larger brand paid me more": ask whether that partnership continued. Then offer a recurring
  arrangement.
- "Sponsored posts hurt my reach": explain that the product appears inside their normal format, not
  as a separate ad. Cite past results only when they are documented.

## Review boundary

Use [the method](../method.md) and [shared rubric](../copy-validation-rubric.md). Do not invent
rapport, results, or other creators' rates. Keep commercial terms, contracts, and payments outside the
drafting authority. All send, enrichment, CRM, and scheduling actions require separate exact action
approval.
