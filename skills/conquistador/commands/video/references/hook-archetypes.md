# Opening choices for a useful short-form artifact

Start with the viewer's task and the evidence available to show. An opening earns its place by setting a promise the asset fulfills. No named framework, platform tier or borrowed timing window determines quality.

## Choose the opening

| Available evidence | Opening choice | What the rest must deliver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A visible task can be performed | Begin with its input or finished output and name the task. | Show the decisive action and any setup the viewer needs. |
| The user must choose between alternatives | State the decision and a constraint. | Compare the alternatives on the same criterion. |
| An authorized measurement supports a claim | Show the result with its scope. | Explain the method, period and limitation behind the result. |
| The audience has a specific unresolved question | Ask that question in plain language. | Answer it completely, or name the explicit boundary of the answer. |

Another opening is acceptable when its intended viewer, evidence and payoff are explicit. Common descriptive terms such as question or demonstration are labels, not proprietary methods or performance claims. Do not require competitor-exclusive wording for an ordinary product task.

## Specify it for production

For each proposed opening, write the exact first frame, visible action, spoken line if any, text overlay and sound state. Give the intended timing in the storyboard. Choose that timing by a read-through and task comprehension, then check it against verified upload constraints. Silence or no overlay is valid when the information remains accessible and understandable.

Produce the alternatives requested by the brief. Change a meaningful decision, such as whether the task or the output appears first. Cosmetic synonyms do not make a useful comparison. Explain why the recommended opening fits the available evidence and production capacity.

## Review the promise

1. Identify the claim an unfamiliar viewer would take from the opening.
2. Point to the exact later frame or source that supports it.
3. Check that the text, speech and visual do not make conflicting claims.
4. Inspect whether a viewer can understand the opening in the intended playback conditions.
5. Remove invented results, hidden conditions and unnecessary setup before the relevant task.

Record an unresolved claim as a blocker. A real customer phrase needs its source and permission; when none is supplied, write plain audience language without presenting it as a quote.

## Test and hand off

Record the changed variable, intended audience, metric definitions, observation window and resource cap. Compare against the account's own suitable baseline when available. Preserve unequal exposure and missing data as limits. Catalog frequency cannot establish that an opening caused performance.

Deliver the selected opening, alternatives, promise-to-evidence mapping, storyboard timings and next test. The output is a draft until production and action review are complete.
