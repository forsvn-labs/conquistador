# Choose an opening that delivers the promised information

Use the reader decision and available evidence from the angle bank. The opening
must introduce what the asset actually supplies. Its label does not predict reach.

Consider a concrete task description, a product demonstration, an observed question,
or a bounded comparison when the evidence supports it. A first-person account needs
a real permitted narrator; a numerical opening needs scoped measurements. Do not
create a personal history, provocative statistic, or disagreement just to fit a
hook category.

Specify the opening words or visual, the evidence that follows, and the action the
reader can take. For video, include readable captions and accessible alternatives.
For a platform, consult the method pack's §1 opening choices and §2 task-verified
constraints. There are no required hook tiers or emotion pairings.

When comparing openings, retain the information promise, proof, audience, and
destination as controls. State the outcome and observation requirement. If other
components also change, label a concept comparison and limit causal conclusions.
Reject an opening that cannot fulfill its promise even if it appears attention-
grabbing. Informational clarity is sufficient when that is the reader's task.
