# Campaign decision examples

These are invented instructional scenarios, not customer evidence, published
campaigns, or benchmarks. Each product fact and resource amount belongs only to
its example. Do not reuse its figures as a default.

## A task with sufficient product evidence

A fictional scheduling product can import an existing appointment list. The
operator wants prospective users to complete an import preview. The supplied
example record contains a demonstration and a question about duplicate handling.

| # | Pillar | Type | % | Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Preview an appointment import | Explain supported behavior | N/A | Preparing an import from an existing list | Example product demonstration |
| P2 | Resolve duplicate entries | Answer a migration question | N/A | Reviewing conflicting records before import | Example duplicate-handling specification |

The tasks can occur in either order and for the same reader. The plan does not
assign a share of the audience to a knowledge stage.

| # | Angle | Hook | Stage | Trigger | Score | Class | Pillar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Preview the imported appointments before confirming | Show the preview | Preparing the first import | User has an existing appointment list | 1 | Product demonstration | P1 |
| A2 | Check which duplicate entry will be retained | Show a conflict and its resolution | Reviewing conflicting records | Duplicate found in the input | 2 | Reference answer | P2 |

For each angle, attach the exact demonstration step, decision, next action, and
rejection condition. A1 is prioritized because it addresses the campaign's immediate
task. A2 remains available for readers who encounter a conflict. Neither needs a
fear, pride, or belonging label to be useful.

## A comparison with insufficient evidence

A proposed angle claims that the product imports faster than every competing tool.
Only the product's own behavior was supplied. Reject the comparison and keep the
supported demonstration. Do not replace the competitor names with anonymous labels
while retaining the unsupported superiority claim.

## A constrained service campaign

A fictional repair service has permission to show its booking process, but no
customer testimonials. The reader needs to decide whether their equipment is
eligible and which information to submit. Generate angles for those questions.
Do not add a testimonial pillar, a customer count, or a false deadline to satisfy
a standard mix. Schedule only assets the service owner can review.

## A repeat user

A user who knows the product asks whether a new export preserves their formatting.
Treat that task directly. Knowledge of the brand does not justify a stronger CTA,
an urgency message, or skipping the demonstration. The useful action may be viewing
a sample export rather than purchasing again.

## Unknown audience access

An operator requests a platform where no audience evidence is supplied. Record
access as unknown and propose the smallest permitted verification step. A method
pack can guide the opening and measurement plan; last_verified:null does not prove
current constraints or expected distribution. pack_verified stays none.

## Capacity and sequence

An example owner has four available production hours. A demonstration requires
three hours and a reference answer requires two. Schedule the demonstration first
or revise scope; do not claim both fit because a two-person team should publish a
certain number of assets. Activation waits for proof, destination readiness, review,
and exact action authority. A later measurement date follows the expected event lag.
