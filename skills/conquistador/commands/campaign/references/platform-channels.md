## Channel decision

1. Read section 0 before selection. Compare its fit/defer conditions to the product, audience, available assets and operator capacity. Record skipped channels with the reason. A material exception needs a justification; it cannot override permission, rights or platform rules.
2. Use section 2 to estimate production and verification work. Set cadence from the team's actual capacity, not a platform duration tier or posting quota.
3. Use section 3 to define how the channel's proposed role can be observed. Assign awareness, trust or conversion using the intended audience action and evidence, not an algorithm signal hierarchy.
4. Trace the action path in section 7. A conversion role requires an available route to the intended destination and a way to distinguish attempted from completed actions.
5. Use sections 5 and 6 to define a bounded test, response coverage and observation window. Give production the selected opening objective and required evidence without prescribing every shot in the campaign plan.

## Execution brief

For each selected channel, record the audience evidence, role, assigned message, asset format, capacity estimate, owner, verified account requirements, next-action route, measurement definitions, baseline or baseline-collection plan, test window and resource cap. State the exact uncertainty that the next test will resolve.

Record `pack_verified: none` until task-local external mechanics have been checked; the method revision date is separate. A missing verification may permit planning but cannot support an upload-ready claim. If no pack fits, label the proposed route unverified and specify the operator check instead of inventing a tailored method.

Campaigns that include source clipping also use `distribution-models/clipping-and-live.md` to check source rights, excerpt context and distribution economics. Do not infer audience fit from a demographic stereotype or an external creator's success story.
