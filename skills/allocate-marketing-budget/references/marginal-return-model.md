# Compare the next bounded allocation

Start with the budget, horizon, objective, reserve, and approved constraints. A channel's historical average cost does not establish what an additional allocation will produce.

## Define comparable return

State the useful outcome and its quality boundary. Match spend and outcomes by population, offer, period, and attribution. Average acquisition cost is included spend divided by attributed customers under those definitions. It is not automatically marginal cost.

Where comparable observations support an incremental estimate, show the change in spend and the associated change in qualified outcomes. Explain confounders such as changed audience, creative, season, or attribution. A before/after difference alone does not prove that extra spend caused the difference.

If incremental response is unknown, keep it unknown. Use clearly labeled user assumptions for scenarios or select learning-budget mode. Do not multiply average cost by an installed saturation factor or infer a channel's spending threshold from its brand.

## Define a useful test floor

Set a minimum commitment from the actual question, observation window, available delivery mechanism, and supplied cost evidence. A placement can have a quoted minimum purchase. A proposed experiment can need a specified observation opportunity. Record the source and uncertainty for either.

There is no installed universal monthly floor or learning-event count. If a meaningful test cannot fit within the budget or measurement plan, allocate zero or propose a different observation. Do not increase spending merely to meet an invented floor.

## Apply readiness checks, section 0

Before comparing allocations, confirm the destination, offer, measurement, permission, and delivery capacity required for each candidate. A missing dependency blocks execution and can make a proposed test unusable. Describe the exact dependency instead of assigning a channel-wide veto from an anecdote.

## Compare alternatives

For each feasible allocation, show amount, plausible outcome range, evidence or assumption, capacity, loss exposure, and information gained. Compare at least the current allocation and a useful alternative. Include reserve explicitly.

Under a stated response model, allocate bounded increments and recalculate return as the assumed response changes. Do not claim a global optimum when response curves are unknown, discontinuous, or interacting. Respect the user's concentration limit and cash constraints.

## Reconcile and recommend

Allocated amounts plus reserve must equal the budget. Define review dates, stop conditions, and the destination of released funds. Recommend the allocation that best serves the stated objective within the evidence limits, or explain why holding funds is preferable.

The output is advisory. Purchasing, bid changes, and movement of funds require human approval. A modeled allocation is not an executed or successful campaign.
