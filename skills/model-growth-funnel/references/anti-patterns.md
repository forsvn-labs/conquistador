# Funnel modeling mistakes

- Mixing people, accounts, orders, and revenue in one transition makes the result uninterpretable. Define the unit and joins first.
- Filling a missing baseline with an industry comparison hides the actual evidence gap. Keep the baseline unavailable and label scenario assumptions separately.
- Choosing a percentage lift from product maturity invents forecast authority. Tie the magnitude to evidence or mark it as a scenario input.
- Increasing an assumed conversion rate to meet a goal conceals the decision. Show the required assumption and test its capacity implications.
- Adding effects from several initiatives can double-count the same users. Explain interactions and use compatible cohorts.
- Treating a target as a measured result misleads downstream planning. Preserve observed, estimated, desired, and unavailable states.
- Applying a universal cost ratio or stretch target ignores the user's constraints. State the actual decision threshold and who owns it.
- Returning only a request for more data leaves the decision unfinished. Offer a bounded scenario, a feasible alternative, or a precise no-go with the next useful observation.
- Calling a local model a successful campaign fabricates execution. Keep live results and approval separate.
