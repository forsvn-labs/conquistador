# Design a pricing decision

Use when choosing pricing, trial access, usage limits, or a change to existing terms.

## Establish the unit being sold

Describe the useful result and the unit the buyer can understand: access, completed work, seats, or measured usage. Check that the billing unit can be measured and explained. Identify costs that increase with usage and any customer obligations after cancellation.

List current terms and proposed terms side by side. Include eligibility, limits, renewal, refunds, and support. Separate new-customer experiments from changes to existing agreements.

## Compare feasible access models

Consider paid access, time-limited trials, restricted free access, or assisted demonstrations according to the product's costs and buying process. Explain the question each option can answer and its abuse or support exposure. No access model is universally correct.

Define a bounded test with eligible users, disclosed terms, measurement window, and decision criteria. Record acceptance, successful use, cancellations, support effort, and collected revenue separately. A higher sign-up count can coexist with worse economics.

## Output

Provide the proposed terms, cost assumptions, test design, and authority required to offer them. State how existing users are protected and how the test ends. Draft changes do not authorize charging or altering subscriptions.

## Example, synthetic

A document service incurs a cost per processed page. Test an explicit page allowance with clear overage terms only if metering and support can enforce it. If they cannot, use a bounded demonstration while the billing decision remains open.

## Limit

A price test measures behavior within its audience and period. Do not generalize willingness to pay from a handful of responses or treat a proposed price as realized revenue.
